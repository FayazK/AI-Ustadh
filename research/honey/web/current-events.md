## Fake / adulterated honey

- **EU "From the Hives" investigation — ~46% of imported honey suspected fake.** The European Commission's coordinated action (with OLAF and the Joint Research Centre) tested 320 honey samples at EU import borders and found 46% suspected of being adulterated with cheap sugar syrups (rice, wheat, beet, etc.) — a sharp jump from 14% in the previous 2015–2017 study. By origin, suspicion rates were ~74% for honey from China, ~93% from Turkey, and 100% (10 of 10) for honey re-exported via the UK (likely from blending before re-export). (European Commission / JRC, "From the Hives," published 2023 — https://food.ec.europa.eu/safety/eu-agri-food-fraud-network/eu-coordinated-actions/honey-2021-2022_en)

- **Honey is one of the most fraud-prone foods on Earth.** Alongside olive oil, fish, and milk, honey is flagged as a high-risk product for "economically motivated adulteration." Fraudsters now design syrups specifically to evade standard tests, so labs rely on advanced methods — NMR spectroscopy and stable carbon isotope ratio analysis (SCIRA / EA-IRMS). Popular home "tests" (water test, thumb test) are not scientifically reliable. (Food-fraud overview; FDA EMA program; FSA — general, ongoing)

- **EU "Breakfast Directives" reform — stricter honey origin labels (Directive (EU) 2024/1438).** Adopted and published in the EU Official Journal in May 2024, the reform forces honey blends to list every country of origin in descending order by weight (ending the vague "blend of EU and non-EU honeys" label) and commits the EU to harmonised testing, a traceability system, and an EU reference laboratory for honey authenticity. Labeling provisions apply from around June 2026. The reform was driven largely by the "From the Hives" findings and pressure from European beekeepers undercut by cheap imports. (EUR-Lex / European Commission, May 2024)

- **UK retailer honey — over 90% of supermarket samples flagged as suspicious (2024).** A Randox Food Diagnostics study tested 30 honeys: of 25 bought from large UK retailers, 24 failed authenticity checks (flagged for likely sugar-syrup dilution), while all 5 sampled directly from British beekeepers were genuine. The result fed intense UK debate about whether cheap blended supermarket honey is really honey. (Randox Food Diagnostics, 2024 — https://randoxfood.com/over-90-of-honey-samples-from-uk-retailers-fail-authenticity-check/)

- **US FDA FY25 testing — ~4% adulteration found.** The FDA's FY2025 sampling assignment tested 102 honey samples (54 domestic, 48 imported) for "economically motivated adulteration" using stable carbon isotope ratio analysis, finding a ~4% violation rate (2 domestic, 2 imported) where cheaper sugarcane/corn syrups had been added. The much lower rate vs. the EU reflects a different (more conservative) test method, not necessarily cleaner honey. (US FDA, FY25 results, 2025 — https://www.fda.gov/food/economically-motivated-adulteration-food-fraud/fy25-sample-collection-and-analysis-domestically-produced-and-imported-honey-economically-motivated)

- **The "testing method" controversy.** There is still no single globally accepted authenticity test, which complicates enforcement and lets disputes fester. The UK Food Standards Agency is developing new DNA-based detection methods (Cranfield proof-of-concept, Feb 2025), and the Global Honey Organization has publicly contested the EU "From the Hives" NMR methodology as prone to false positives — so even the headline fraud numbers are contested. (FSA blog Feb 2025; Global Honey Organization, 2023–2025)

## Manuka honey market (premium pricing & authentication)

- **A booming, heavily-counterfeited premium market.** The global Manuka honey market is valued at roughly USD 0.6–0.8 billion and is projected to exceed USD 1.5 billion by 2030 (CAGR ~8–12%), driven by wellness demand. Far more "Manuka honey" is sold worldwide than New Zealand actually produces — a clear sign of widespread counterfeiting. (Grand View Research / industry reports, 2024)

- **Grading and authentication.** Premium pricing tracks two grading systems: **UMF (Unique Manuka Factor)**, based on markers like leptosperin, DHA and methylglyoxal (typically 5+ to 24+), and **MGO (methylglyoxal in mg/kg)**, e.g. 100+, 250+, 550+, 850+. To fight fakes, New Zealand's Ministry for Primary Industries requires exported Manuka to pass a five-attribute test (four chemical markers plus a DNA marker from manuka pollen). (NZ MPI / market reports, ongoing)

## Bee decline / pollinator crisis

- **Worst US honeybee die-off on record (2024–2025).** US commercial beekeepers reported catastrophic colony losses — roughly 62% on average between June 2024 and early 2025, with some operations losing 60%+ — described as one of the worst die-offs ever recorded. (USDA ARS / Project Apis m. / Bee Informed Partnership / Cornell, 2025)

- **Causes under investigation.** Early findings point to viral diseases spread by *Varroa destructor* mites (deformed wing virus, acute bee paralysis virus), with concern that varroa is developing resistance to amitraz, a widely-used miticide; nutritional stress, forage loss, pesticides and climate are also implicated. (USDA ARS / Project Apis m., 2025)

- **Why it matters beyond honey.** Commercial colonies are trucked across the US each spring to pollinate crops — California's almond industry is especially dependent — so mass losses threaten food supply and farm economics, not just honey output. (Industry / USDA reporting, 2025)

## Sugar & diabetes epidemic (honey as a "natural sweetener")

- **WHO counts honey as "free sugar" — same limits as table sugar.** WHO advises keeping free sugars under 10% of energy intake, ideally under 5% (~25 g / 6 tsp a day). Crucially, "free sugars" explicitly include the sugars in honey, syrups and fruit juice — so the popular "natural sweetener" framing does not exempt honey from the limit. (WHO sugar guideline, ongoing)

- **Global diabetes has more than quadrupled — over 800 million adults.** A November 2024 Lancet study (NCD-RisC, co-authored with WHO, pooling 1,108 studies / 141 million participants) found more than 800 million adults were living with diabetes in 2022 — over four times the ~200 million of 1990. Roughly 445 million adults 30+ with diabetes (about 59%) were taking no medication, with the worst treatment gaps in low- and middle-income countries. (The Lancet / Imperial College London, Nov 2024 — https://www.imperial.ac.uk/news/258586/more-than-800-million-adults-living/)

- **WHO's renewed push on sugar taxes (Jan 2026).** In January 2026 the WHO again urged governments to sharply raise taxes on sugary drinks (and alcohol), warning that such products are too cheap and too accessible and are fueling obesity, diabetes and other NCDs; over 100 jurisdictions already levy some sugar-sweetened-beverage tax. WHO's earlier 2023 guidance also advised against non-sugar sweeteners (aspartame, stevia, sucralose) for weight control — together these trends help marketers reposition honey as the "natural, healthier" sweetener, even though WHO still counts it as free sugar. (UN News / WHO, Jan 2026 — https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/01/1166754)

## Honey health-trend culture (social media & wellness claims)

- **Raw honey / immunity hype.** Raw, unfiltered honey is heavily promoted on social media for "immunity-boosting," antibacterial, antioxidant, allergy-relief and gut-health benefits. Evidence is genuinely supportive for one use — soothing coughs/sore throats (recommended in some pediatric guidance) — but most broader claims outpace the science. (Health-expert commentary, ongoing)

- **"Frozen honey" viral trend.** A TikTok trend (viral ~2021, periodically resurfacing) has people freezing honey and eating it like a chewy gummy; experts warn large quantities cause cramps and diarrhea due to the sheer sugar load. (Health reporting, 2021–ongoing)

- **Persistent safety caveats experts repeat.** Honey must never be given to infants under 12 months (infant botulism risk), and "natural" does not mean unlimited — honey is still sugar. (Pediatric / public-health guidance, ongoing)
