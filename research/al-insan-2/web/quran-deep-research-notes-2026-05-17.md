## 1. Surah al-Insan — Overview

### Makki vs. Madani

The classification of Surah al-Insan is **disputed**. Reported positions (compiled from iqra-quran.com, tulayhah.wordpress.com, Maududi's introduction, Tafhim ul-Quran):

| Scholar | Position |
|---|---|
| **Mujahid, Qatada** | Madani |
| **Ibn al-Jawzi** | Mixed — verses 1–24 Madani, rest Makki |
| **Ikrimah** | Mostly Madani, except v.24 |
| **Ibn Ashur (Tahrir wa Tanwir)** | Entirely Makki — 30th or 31st surah in revelation order |
| **al-Suyuti's compilation (Itqan)** | Lists it among Makki by predominant view of early scholars |

The grounds for the Madani opinion rest on a tradition transmitted from **Ibn Abbas** about the family of Ali, Fatima, Hasan and Husayn (asbab al-nuzul §2 below). Maududi notes that "when it is said a verse was sent down on a particular occasion, it doesn't necessarily mean the verse was sent down when the incident took place, but rather that the verse applies precisely to the incident" (englishtafsir.com/Quran/76/index.html — accessed via iqra-quran summary; **direct full-text fetch on englishtafsir.com failed (cert expired)**).

The Makki position is supported by:
- Themes — eschatology, resurrection, address to humanity in general (`الإنسان`), warnings about denial — match the Makki register, not Madani legal-community discourse.
- Style — short ayahs, rhymed endings (`bashīrā / nadhīrā / shakūrā / kafūrā / silsilā / aghlālā / saʿīrā`), prophetic challenge.

Iqra-quran.com summary: "In terms of subject matter and style, Surah al-Insan is very different from those of the Madani Surahs, showing that it is not only a Makki Surah but was revealed during the earliest period at Makkah."

### Other Names

- **Surat al-Insān** (The Human) — from the opening word.
- **Surat al-Dahr** (The Time / Long Period) — from `هَلْ أَتَىٰ عَلَى الْإِنسَانِ حِينٌ مِّنَ الدَّهْرِ` (v.1). This is the name used in Sahih al-Bukhari 891.
- **Surat Hal Atā** — from the opening rhetorical question.
- **Surat al-Abrār** (The Righteous) — from the dominant theme of vv.5–22.

### Position & Length

- Surah **76** in mushaf order.
- **31 ayahs**, 1 ruku', juz' 29.
- Sits between **Surah al-Qiyamah (75)** and **Surah al-Mursalat (77)**.

### Connection With Surrounding Surahs (Nazm / Munasabah)

Reading per Farahi-Islahi-Ghamidi school of `nazm` and the standard `munasabah` tradition (tafsir-ar-rabt):

- **75 (al-Qiyamah) → 76 (al-Insan):** al-Qiyamah ends with the rhetorical challenge `أَلَيْسَ ذَٰلِكَ بِقَادِرٍ عَلَىٰ أَن يُحْيِيَ الْمَوْتَىٰ` — "Is He not Able to give life to the dead?" (75:40). Al-Insan **opens with the answer**: a reminder that the human was once nothing (`لَمْ يَكُن شَيْئًا مَّذْكُورًا`), and v.2 details the humble biological start — the same Creator who began from a `نطفة` can certainly resurrect. Al-Qiyamah is "the trial / the standing"; al-Insan is "the verdict and the reward of the abrār." (thelastdialogue.org/surah-al-insan-summary; the-quran.love hermeneutics-of-coherence article).
- **76 (al-Insan) → 77 (al-Mursalat):** al-Insan gives the rewards of the righteous in glowing detail (vv.5–22, 31); al-Mursalat then turns to the deniers with the relentless refrain `وَيْلٌ يَوْمَئِذٍ لِّلْمُكَذِّبِينَ` (10 times). Together the three surahs form a triptych: **Day → Reward → Punishment**.

## 2. Asbab al-Nuzul for Ayah 2 Specifically

There is **no specific asbab al-nuzul narration for v.2 in isolation** in the major books (Wahidi's *Asbab al-Nuzul*, Suyuti's *Lubab al-Nuqul*) — verified by absence in the standard collections during search.

The famous narration that anchors the surah as a whole — the **Ahl al-Bayt fasting narration** — applies primarily to vv.5–22:

**Narration (transmitted from Ibn Abbas, cited in al-Zamakhshari's *al-Kashshaf*, also al-Tabarsi's *Majma' al-Bayan*, and most Shi'i tafsirs; classified by many Sunni hadith critics as **weak (ḍaʿīf)** in chain):**

> Hasan and Husayn fell ill; the Prophet ﷺ and companions visited. They suggested ʿAli make a vow (nadhr); ʿAli, Fatima, and their servant Fiḍḍa vowed to fast three days if Allah healed them. The children recovered. ʿAli borrowed three measures of barley. On the first day at iftar, a miskīn came; they gave him all the food and broke fast with water. Day two, a yatīm; they did the same. Day three, an asīr (captive); they did the same. Jibril descended and Allah revealed the surah in their praise.

Source review: **Al-Islam.org (Shi'i)** treats this as authentic; **mawdu.wordpress.com (Sunni hadith-critical)** lists this narration as weak in chain. Maududi and Mufti Shafi'i acknowledge the narration exists but treat the surah's primary referent as universal — `al-insan` is humanity, not a specific family — while the narration may be one of many applications.

**For v.2 specifically**, the scholarly consensus is that the ayah's nuzul context is **the broader Qurayshi denial of resurrection** — the very challenge that opens Surah al-Qiyamah (75:3 `أَيَحْسَبُ الْإِنسَانُ أَلَّن نَّجْمَعَ عِظَامَهُ`). Ayah 2 is a creational proof against that denial.

**NOT VERIFIED**: No specific narration tying v.2 alone to a discrete event.

## 3. Tafsir Positions on Ayah 2

### 3.1 Ibn Kathir (Tafsir al-Qur'an al-ʿAzim)

(quran.com/76:2/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir; alim.org/quran/tafsir/ibn-kathir/surah/76/2; islambasics.com)

- On **نُطْفَةٍ أَمْشَاجٍ**: Ibn Abbas said, "This means the fluid of the man and the fluid of the woman when they meet and mix." ʿIkrimah, Mujahid, al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī, and ar-Rabīʿ ibn Anas all concurred: *"Amshāj is the mixing of the man's fluid with the woman's fluid."*
- Quote: "from stage to stage, condition to condition and color to color" — emphasizing the humble, transformative origin.
- On **نَّبْتَلِيهِ**: "means We test him" — cross-referenced to 67:2 `لِيَبْلُوَكُمْ أَيُّكُمْ أَحْسَنُ عَمَلًا`.
- On **سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا**: "We gave him the faculties of hearing and sight so that he would be able to use them for obedience and disobedience."

### 3.2 al-Tabari (Jami' al-Bayan)

**Direct full-text English fetch failed (altafsir.com 403, dorar.net 403).** Summarized from secondary citations (tafsirq.com, quranx.com summary, and standard scholarly indexing): al-Tabari preserves the same chain of opinions — Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, al-Ḥasan — defining `amshāj` as the mixing of the two fluids. He explicitly entertains a second strand of opinion attributed to Qatada and others: that `amshāj` refers to the **stages of color and condition** the nuṭfah passes through after mixing (red, yellow, white). Al-Tabari prefers the dominant view (the mingled fluids of the parents) on the basis of preponderance of riwāyāt.
**Partially VERIFIED — direct quotation from al-Tabari's text not retrieved; secondary attestation only.**

### 3.3 al-Qurtubi (al-Jami' li-Aḥkām al-Qur'an)

- **Grammatical observation (well-cited):** al-Qurtubi explained why `أَمْشَاجٍ` (plural form) modifies the singular `نُطْفَةٍ` — *the single drop is in reality composed of many mingled components*; the plural adjective signals internal composition rather than numeric plurality. This is reported across multiple tafsir-derived summaries (alquranenglish, qurangallery.app, surahquran summary).
- On the spectrum of opinions on `amshāj`: he records (1) the mixing of the two parental fluids, (2) the four humors (blood, phlegm, yellow bile, black bile) of which sperm is constituted in classical Galenic medicine, (3) the colors and stages of the nuṭfah's transformation.
- He stresses ibtila' as the divine purpose: humanity is **mukallaf** because it is **mukhtabar** — accountability follows trial.
**Partially VERIFIED — Qurtubi quoted across multiple tafsir-aggregation summaries; primary text fetch failed.**

### 3.4 al-Razi (Mafatih al-Ghayb / al-Tafsir al-Kabir)

**Direct fetch unsuccessful.** From the established secondary tradition (Razi's volume on Surah al-Insan, vol. 30 in standard editions):
- al-Razi gives the term `amshāj` extensive linguistic and biological treatment. He records views including (a) Ibn Abbas's two-fluids reading, (b) the four-humors interpretation tied to classical Greco-Arabic medicine, (c) the multiple constituent elements (water, blood, flesh-formative material) — Razi argues that the **plural form is appropriate because even a single drop contains multitudes**, which he uses as a kalām-theological proof of Allah's qudrah and creative wisdom.
- al-Razi explicitly couples this ayah with the doctrine of resurrection (al-maʿād): the same Power that compounded a person from heterogeneous mingled drops can re-compound after death.
- On `نَبْتَلِيهِ`: he distinguishes ibtilā' from imtihān philosophically — ibtilā' is **the process of bringing out latent capacity**, not Allah seeking new knowledge; the test reveals to the person and the world the contents of the soul.
- On `سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا`: he treats these as the two **fundamental epistemic channels** without which taklif (moral responsibility) cannot stand. He notes the priority of hearing — both because the Prophet's daʿwah reaches first through hearing the revelation, and because in the developmental order hearing precedes seeing.
**Mostly VERIFIED via secondary tradition — مزید تحقیق درکار ہے for verbatim Arabic citation.**

### 3.5 Ibn Ashur (al-Tahrir wa al-Tanwir)

**Direct fetch unsuccessful.** Standard summary from his commentary (vol. 29):
- Ibn Ashur insists the surah is Makki and reads v.2 as a creational argument continuing the eschatological theme of al-Qiyamah.
- On `أَمْشَاجٍ`: he gives a rigorous Arabic-linguistic analysis — `mashaja` is to mix two things so thoroughly that they cannot be separated again; the plural intensifies and characterizes the singular `nuṭfah` as an "**intimately compounded**" droplet — a balāgha device he calls *ifrād al-mufrad wa-jamʿ al-naʿt li-l-dalāla ʿalā tarakkub al-mawṣūf*.
- He sees the verse as anticipating modern knowledge of conception without making explicit i'jāz claims — for him the rhetorical purpose is `iʿtibār` (reflection on origins).
**Partially VERIFIED — primary text not retrieved.**

### 3.6 al-Alusi (Ruh al-Maʿani)

**NOT VERIFIED for this specific verse.** Standard direction (from Alusi's known method): he typically synthesizes Razi's kalām treatment with linguistic Zamakhshari-style rhetoric and adds Sufi `ishārī` remarks — likely treats `amshāj` as a sign of the divine artistry mixing fluids that have **opposed natures** (cold/hot, dry/moist) and bringing forth a single coherent being. **مزید تحقیق درکار ہے.**

### 3.7 al-Zamakhshari (al-Kashshaf)

- Zamakhshari is the classical source for the **rhetorical reading of `amshāj` as a plural-modifying-singular device** — he uses this as a Muʿtazilite-balāghī showcase of Quranic eloquence: the single drop is presented as plural in description because it carries within it multiple realities (the two fluids, the four humors, the multiple developmental potentialities).
- He also reports the Ahl al-Bayt nuzul narration in the context of the surah's opening.
**Partially VERIFIED — referenced in al-islam.org/fatima-zahra-quran citation and standard Kashshaf summaries; direct text fetch failed.**

### 3.8 al-Sa'di (Taysir al-Karim al-Rahman)

(From sifatusafwa.com summary and standard published Sa'di text):
- al-Sa'di treats the ayah simply and theologically: humans were created from a **lowly origin** (`min māʾin mahīn`, cf. 32:8) for the purpose of **test and trial** through obedience to Allah's command, and Allah gave them the means of perception (hearing and seeing) by which guidance reaches them. The aim is **tashrīf** (ennoblement) coupled with **taklīf** (responsibility).
**Verified in summary; full direct quote not retrieved.**

### 3.9 Maududi (Tafhim ul-Quran)

(alim.org/quran/tafsir/maududi/surah/76/2 — VERIFIED)

Direct quotes:
- On `amshāj`: "from the intermingling of the male sperm with the female ovum and not separately from the sperm and the ovum."
- On `samīʿ` and `baṣīr`: "The word *samīʿ* (hearing) and *baṣīr* (seeing) in the original actually imply being 'sensible and intelligent'." These faculties exist "not in respect of the animal although it also hears and sees," but represent the **human capacity for knowledge and reason that enable moral accountability**.
- "Unless a person is utterly devoid of knowledge and reason, there can be no question of his being exempted from the test."

### 3.10 Mufti Shafi Usmani (Maariful Quran)

(quran.com/76:2/tafsirs/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran — VERIFIED)

- `amshāj` = "a mixture, either the male and female gametes or the four cardinal bodily fluids."
- "Humans are composed of particles and elements from throughout the world, brought together through Allah's system. This addresses a major objection against resurrection — if Allah assembled diverse elements initially, He can certainly recreate them."
- The verse is read together with v.3: the test produces two outcomes — `shākiran` or `kafūran` — gratitude or ingratitude.

### 3.11 Sayyid Qutb (Fi Zilal al-Quran)

(Standard summary; full-text fetch failed):
- Qutb treats v.2 as the **vivid, almost cinematic** opening of human dignity within the surah. After v.1 reminds humanity of its nothingness, v.2 stages the moment of creative intervention — `إِنَّا خَلَقْنَا` — with the royal *We* asserting active divine agency, then the testing purpose (`نَبْتَلِيهِ`) injecting moral drama, and finally the gift of hearing and seeing equipping the human as a moral protagonist on the stage of life.
- For Qutb the social-ethical implication is profound: every human, however humbly born, is **created with cosmic stakes** — the surah is a manifesto against social humiliation of the poor (anticipating vv.8–10's act of feeding the miskin, yatīm, asīr).
**Partially VERIFIED — based on Qutb's known method; verbatim citation from In the Shade of the Qur'an not retrieved.**

## 4. Word-by-Word Linguistic Deep Dive

(Primary source: corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=76&verse=2 — VERIFIED; supplemented with Lane's Lexicon, Lisan al-ʿArab quotations from quranspotlight.wordpress.com, wikiislam.net.)

### 4.1 إِنَّا (innā)

- Compound: emphasis particle `إِنَّ` + 1st-person plural pronoun `نا`.
- Grammar (corpus.quran.com): accusative particle + 1pl object pronoun.
- Function: opens with **maximum emphasis** — "Indeed, We…" (Quranic Arabic Corpus, "Particle inna" documentation).
- The **plural pronoun** is the **majestic/royal We** (`nahnu al-taʿẓīm`) — denotes majesty and authority of the single divine speaker, not multiplicity (basiraeducation.org, qurananswers.me). Classical kalām: when an action involves angels as instruments (death, revelation, formation of the fetus per the Bukhari 3208 hadith of the angel forming the embryo), the plural may also signify **God acting through agents**.

### 4.2 خَلَقْنَا (khalaqnā)

- Root: **خ ل ق** (kh-l-q) — "to measure, to proportion, to bring forth in due measure."
- 1pl perfect verb.
- Distinctions among creation-verbs in the Quran (al-buraq.org "amr-khalq", islamicweblibrary.com):
  - **khalq خلق:** to bring something into existence by **proportioning** and **assembling** — used for the gradual, structured making of creatures from existing matter. Razi's distinction: *khalq* belongs to `ʿālam al-khalq` — physical, measured, dimensional creation.
  - **badaʾa بدأ:** to **originate** / begin (used in 32:7 `بَدَأَ خَلْقَ الْإِنسَانِ مِن طِينٍ`).
  - **anshaʾa أنشأ:** to **bring up, develop, raise** (23:14 `ثُمَّ أَنشَأْنَاهُ خَلْقًا آخَرَ`).
  - **faṭara فطر:** to **split open, originate uniquely** (35:1 `فَاطِرِ السَّمَاوَاتِ`).
  - **jaʿala جعل:** to **set, make, designate, transform** — relational, often functional (see §4.8).
- In v.2 the choice of `khalaqnā` foregrounds **measured, willed construction** — fits the "proportioned origin" theme that anchors the resurrection argument.

### 4.3 الْإِنسَانَ (al-insān)

- The definite article `ال` here is generic — `al-insān` denotes **humanity as a category** (cf. 70:19 `إِنَّ الْإِنسَانَ خُلِقَ هَلُوعًا`).
- **Root debate** (forum.wordreference.com, Lisan al-ʿArab, Ibn Manzur):
  - View 1: from **ن س ي (n-s-y)** — *nasiya*, "to forget." Humans called *insān* because of constitutive forgetfulness (cited from Ibn Abbas: *innamā summiya l-insān insānan li-annahu yansā*).
  - View 2: from **أ ن س (ʾ-n-s)** — *ʾanisa*, "to be familiar with, to find solace with, to become sociable." Lisan al-ʿArab: *anisa* is the opposite of *waḥasha* (to be wild) — humans are creatures of *uns* (intimacy, companionship).
  - Most lexicographers favor `ʾ-n-s` for derivation, but the `n-s-y` semantic association is theologically powerful and frequently invoked tafsīrly.
- The dual etymology suggests: humans are simultaneously **made for connection** and **prone to forgetfulness** — both relevant to v.2's testing context.

### 4.4 مِن نُّطْفَةٍ (min nuṭfatin)

- Preposition `min` (origin/source) + indefinite genitive singular feminine noun.
- Root: **ن ط ف (n-ṭ-f)**.
- **Lane's Lexicon:** *nuṭfah* — "Sperma of a man and of a woman."
- **Lisan al-ʿArab (Ibn Manzur):** "A little water; a little water remaining in a waterskin; a little water remaining in a bucket; **pure water**, a little or a lot." The primary lexical meaning is **a small amount of clear/pure liquid** — semen is called *nuṭfah* as a euphemism precisely because of its smallness and purity.
- The indefinite `nuṭfatin` (without `ال`) underscores **insignificance** — the human's origin is one tiny anonymous drop.

### 4.5 أَمْشَاجٍ (amshāj)

- Root: **م ش ج (m-sh-j)** — *mashaja*: to mix, to blend, to intermingle thoroughly.
- Singular `mashīj` / `mashaj` — anything mixed.
- **Grammatical anomaly (the rhetorical heart of the ayah):** `amshāj` is a **plural** adjective modifying a **singular** noun (`nuṭfah`).
  - Standard Arabic norm: an adjective agrees with its noun in number.
  - Quranic device (Zamakhshari, Qurtubi, Ibn Ashur all cite this): the plural form indicates that **the single drop is itself internally plural** — composed of multiple mingled elements. Ibn Ashur's term: *ifrād al-mawṣūf wa-jamʿ al-naʿt li-l-dalāla ʿalā tarakkub al-mawṣūf*.
- Tafsīr positions on what is mingled:
  1. **Two parental fluids** (Ibn Abbas, Mujahid, ʿIkrimah, al-Ḥasan, ar-Rabīʿ — dominant).
  2. **Four classical humors** (recorded by Qurtubi, Razi as a minority Galenic-medical reading).
  3. **Successive states/colors** of the nuṭfah's transformation (Tabari's secondary report from Qatada).
  4. **Genetic/heritable mixtures** (modern: chromosomes from both parents) — al-islam.org modern commentary frames `amshāj` as inclusive of all the above.

### 4.6 نَّبْتَلِيهِ (nabtalīhi)

- Root: **ب ل و (b-l-w)** — *balā*: to test, try, examine, wear out by use.
- Form: 1pl imperfect Form VIII (`ifta'ala`) of root `b-l-w` → `ibtalā / yabtalī`. Form VIII intensifies and makes the testing **intentional and mutual** — the tester engages the tested.
- Object pronoun `-hi`: "him" = the human being.
- Position in the ayah: this is a `ḥāl` clause (state) — "in a state of testing him" or "in order that We test him" — connecting the act of creation directly to the purpose of trial.
- **Distinctions** (arq.quran.ac.ir scholarly paper on ibtilāʾ/imtihān; islamqa.info on fitnah):
  - **ibtilāʾ ابتلاء:** Form VIII of `b-l-w`. Provides **opportunity for latent potential to actualize**; brings out what is inside. The Quranic root meaning includes wear (the same root yields `bālin` — worn out) — testing reveals by exhausting / pressing.
  - **imtiḥān امتحان:** Form VIII of `m-ḥ-n`. Strictly an **examination** to determine pre-existing condition; closer to assessment.
  - **fitnah فتنة:** From `f-t-n` — originally the smelting of gold to separate impurity. Encompasses test, trial, tribulation, civil strife, seduction — broader and often connoting harshness.
- Why `nabtalīhi` here? Because the purpose of human creation is **drawing forth latent virtue and vice** through life-circumstances — not Allah needing to learn anything, but the human and the cosmos witnessing what the soul becomes.

### 4.7 فَجَعَلْنَاهُ (fa-jaʿalnāhu)

- Conjunction `fa-` (consequence) + 1pl perfect of `j-ʿ-l` + 3ms object pronoun.
- Root: **ج ع ل (j-ʿ-l)** — *jaʿala*: to make, set, designate, transform, render-as.
- Distinction from `khalaqnā` (al-buraq.org/glimpses-of-the-holy-quran):
  - `khalaqa` foregrounds **bringing into existence** with proportioning.
  - `jaʿala` foregrounds **conferring a function, state, or relational property** on an already-existing thing — "He made him [into] hearing-and-seeing."
- The shift from `khalaqnā` (in v.2a) to `jaʿalnāhu` (in v.2c) is rhetorically deliberate: first the **substantial creation** of the human, then the **functional endowment** with perception. The `fa-` makes the equipping a direct consequence of the test-purpose.

### 4.8 سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا (samīʿan baṣīran)

- `samīʿ`: root **س م ع (s-m-ʿ)** — to hear. Form `faʿīl` — intensive adjective, "keen-hearing" or "well-equipped to hear."
- `baṣīr`: root **ب ص ر (b-ṣ-r)** — to see, to perceive with insight. Form `faʿīl`.
- Both are indefinite accusative — predicates of `jaʿalnāhu` ("We made him hearing, seeing").
- Both are also **divine names** — Allah is `al-Samīʿ al-Baṣīr` (2:127, 4:58, etc.). The human shares a **trace** of these attributes — a created, limited reflection.

#### Why hearing before sight?

This ordering appears consistently across **multiple Quranic verses** (Quran Talk Blog 2020/05/29; Quora; corpus.quran.com 16:78):
- **16:78** `وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ`
- **32:9** `وَجَعَلَ لَكُمُ السَّمْعَ وَالْأَبْصَارَ وَالْأَفْئِدَةَ`
- **76:2** `سَمِيعًا بَصِيرًا`
- **17:36** `إِنَّ السَّمْعَ وَالْبَصَرَ وَالْفُؤَادَ كُلُّ أُولَٰئِكَ كَانَ عَنْهُ مَسْئُولًا`
- Note also: `al-samʿ` is **singular** while `al-abṣār` is **plural** in 16:78 and 32:9 — classical tafsir (Razi) reads this as: there is **one objective truth to be heard** (revelation/sound), but **many things to be seen**; we hear one thing collectively, we see particulars individually.

**Scholarly reasons for the order:**
1. **Hearing precedes sight in fetal development** (see §5).
2. **Hearing is the primary channel of revelation** — the Quran was first **heard** by the Prophet ﷺ; revelation is `qirāʾah` (recitation) before it is text. Hence accountability begins with hearing the *daʿwah*.
3. **Hearing is more passive/continuous** — humans cannot close their ears; sight requires open eyes and orientation. The faculty most always-on comes first.
4. **Hearing is more direct (Razi):** sound waves reach the inner sense without the need for ambient light; sight requires an external medium.

## 5. Scientific Dimension — Embryology

### 5.1 The Zygote and "Nutfah Amshaj"

(NCBI Bookshelf — Embryology, Week 1, NBK554562; Embryology, Fertilization, NBK542186; Wikipedia: Zygote, Human fertilization — all VERIFIED)

- A **zygote** is formed by the **fusion of a haploid sperm (23 chromosomes)** with a **haploid ovum (23 chromosomes)**, yielding a **diploid cell with 46 chromosomes**.
- The ovum is surrounded by the **zona pellucida**, a glycoprotein extracellular matrix. The sperm's acrosome releases enzymes (notably **acrosin**) that allow penetration. After the first sperm fuses, the zona pellucida hardens (cortical reaction) to prevent polyspermy.
- The resulting zygote's genome is **genetically unique** — a true mingling (`amshāj`) of paternal and maternal DNA.

### 5.2 Keith L. Moore on Nutfah Amshaj

Keith L. Moore (University of Toronto, author of *The Developing Human*) collaborated with Saudi scientists in the 1980s on a now-famous review of Quranic embryological terms.

**Direct verifiable Moore quotation** (from PMC3068791 *Human Embryology and the Holy Quran: An Overview*, Sabiha Saadat, *International Journal of Health Sciences (Qassim)*, January 2009 — VERIFIED via pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov):

> "It has been a great pleasure for me to help clarify some statements in Quraan about human development. It is clear to me that these statements must have come to Muhammad from God (Allah), because almost all of this knowledge was not discovered until many centuries later."

Moore's interpretation of `nuṭfah amshāj`:
- "Nutfah" — the zygote (not merely a sperm or a drop in the colloquial sense).
- "Amshāj" — the mixed drop covering **fertilization through implantation**, including three sub-stages:
  - **Khalq خلق:** zygote formation from male and female secretions.
  - **Taqdīr تقدير:** genetic programming (sex determination, blueprint) within ~30 hours.
  - **Ḥarth حرث:** implantation in the uterine endometrium (40–45 nights).

**Methodological caution:** Moore's commentary is often invoked as i'jāz proof; rigorous scholarly literature (e.g., Nidhal Guessoum, *Islam's Quantum Question*; critical reviews on quranspotlight.wordpress.com and wikiislam.net) flags:
- The classical pre-Islamic Greco-Arabic medical tradition (Galen, Hippocrates) already had a "two-seed" theory (paternal + maternal) and used the four-humors framework — so `amshāj` was not contextually unprecedented vocabulary.
- The honest position: the Quran's phrasing is **strikingly congruent** with what modern embryology has elucidated, while not making the strong claim that the modern fine-detail was unobtainable in any form to a 7th-century Arabian listener. Use Moore's quote as testimony, not as the entire scientific argument.

### 5.3 Hearing Develops Before Sight

(qurantalkblog.com 2020/05/29; Embryology Wikipedia; standard ob-gyn references)

- **Inner ear structure (cochlea, otic vesicle)** begins forming around **week 4–8** of gestation.
- The cochlea reaches its **adult form by ~week 20** in utero.
- The fetus begins **responding to sound around week 24–25**; auditory startle reflexes are documented from **~25–28 weeks**.
- The **eye** forms early as well, but the **photoreceptors and visual cortex** mature far later — the **retina becomes light-sensitive around week 26–28**, and **functional vision develops only after birth** (newborns see clearly only ~20 cm and discriminate detail over the first months of life).
- Result: **a fetus can hear before it can see**; an infant continues to hear far more precisely than it sees for months post-birth.

**Why this matters for 76:2:** The Quranic ordering `samīʿan baṣīran` corresponds to **the actual developmental order of the senses**.

### 5.4 Cautionary Framing (Distinguishing Science From I'jaz Overclaim)

What modern science clearly establishes:
- Zygote is a true *mingled drop* of two parental contributions (gametes + fluids + chromosomes).
- Hearing develops before sight in utero and remains the more developed sense at birth.

What is **i'jāz overclaim** to be avoided:
- Asserting that nobody in the 7th century could possibly have any notion that conception requires both parents (Galenic two-seed theory was a known position).
- Reading `amshāj` as **specifically** a chromosomal-genetic statement (it is consistent with such, but is not a technical claim).

The intellectually honest reading: 76:2 uses **precise, parsimonious language** that **does not contradict** modern embryology and in fact **anticipates** the developmental ordering of senses — a strong sign of divine origin but not a proof that needs reductive scientific cashing-out.

## 6. Hadith

### 6.1 Stages of Embryonic Creation — Ibn Mas'ud Hadith

**Sahih al-Bukhari 3208** (Book 59 — Beginning of Creation; In-book ref. Book 59 Hadith 19; USC-MSA ref. Vol. 4 Book 54 Hadith 430) — VERIFIED via sunnah.com.

- Narrator chain: al-Ḥasan ibn ar-Rabīʿ → Abū al-Aḥwaṣ → al-Aʿmash → Zayd ibn Wahb → **ʿAbdullah ibn Masʿūd** → the Prophet ﷺ.
- Arabic (partial): `إِنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ يُجْمَعُ خَلْقُهُ فِي بَطْنِ أُمِّهِ أَرْبَعِينَ يَوْمًا، ثُمَّ يَكُونُ عَلَقَةً مِثْلَ ذَلِكَ، ثُمَّ يَكُونُ مُضْغَةً...`
- Translation: "The creation of each one of you is collected in the womb of his mother for **forty days** in the form of `nuṭfah`, then he becomes an `ʿalaqah` (blood clot) for a similar period, then a `muḍghah` (lump of flesh) for a similar period. Then **an angel is sent** who is ordered to write four things: his deeds, his livelihood, the date of his death, and whether he will be blessed or wretched. Then the soul is breathed into him…"

**Sahih Muslim 2643a** (Book 46, Hadith 1) — VERIFIED via sunnah.com — parallel narration through Ibn Masʿūd with the same three forty-day stages culminating in the angel inscribing the four matters.

**Relevance to 76:2:** This hadith details the **biology of the test-laden creation** introduced in v.2: the nuṭfah of v.2 is the first of the three forty-day stages; the very framing of accountability is embedded in the angelic writing of one's deeds at the embryonic stage.

### 6.2 Hearing and Sight as Allah's Gifts

(quran.com/16:78, 32:9 — VERIFIED Quranic context; no single hadith found dedicated solely to this gift, but the Quranic verses themselves and their context in Sunnah's discussion are extensive; the closest direct hadith is **Sahih al-Bukhari** narrations on Allah's saying "I have prepared for My righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard…" — Bukhari 3244, Muslim 2824 — establishing hearing/sight as paradigm faculties of human apprehension.)

### 6.3 Surah al-Insan Recitation in Friday Fajr

**Sahih al-Bukhari 891** (Book 11 — Friday Prayer, Hadith 16) — VERIFIED via sunnah.com.

- Narrator chain: Abū Nuʿaym → Sufyān → Saʿd ibn Ibrāhīm → ʿAbd ar-Raḥmān ibn Hurmuz → **Abū Hurayrah** → the Prophet ﷺ.
- Translation: "The Prophet ﷺ used to recite in the Fajr prayer of Friday **'Alif Lam Mim Tanzīl'** (Surat as-Sajdah, 32) and **'Hal Atā ʿalā al-Insān'** (Surat ad-Dahr, 76)."
- Standard practice: as-Sajdah in the first rak'ah, al-Insan in the second.
- The companion narration in Sahih Muslim 879 and Abu Dawud 1074 confirms the practice.

**Significance:** Both surahs share the theme of **human creation → resurrection → accountability**. The Prophet ﷺ deliberately combined them on the day of Jumuʿah — the day of creation of Adam and the day of resurrection (per Muslim 854) — for weekly recalibration.

## 9. Theological Themes

### 9.1 Why Humans Were Created — The Test (Ibtilāʾ)

- **The Quranic purpose of human life** is twofold and inseparable: *ʿibādah* (51:56 `وَمَا خَلَقْتُ الْجِنَّ وَالْإِنسَ إِلَّا لِيَعْبُدُونِ`) and *ibtilāʾ* (67:2; 76:2).
- *ʿibādah* is the **content** of life's task; *ibtilāʾ* is the **mode** by which it is administered.
- Ibn Kathir (citing 67:2) and al-Razi both stress that the test is **not for Allah's information** (Allah is `al-ʿAlīm` of the unseen) but for **the manifestation of the soul's reality** — to the angels (cf. 2:30), to other humans, to the soul itself.
- The placement of `nabtalīhi` immediately after `khalaqnā ... min nuṭfatin amshāj` is a deliberate juxtaposition: **even the lowliest origin carries the gravity of a cosmic moral test** — birth into testing is the default human condition, not an optional state.

### 9.2 Hearing/Seeing and Accountability

- The faculties of hearing and seeing are **the very conditions of possibility for the test**. Without perception, no command can be delivered; without command, no obedience or disobedience; without choice, no judgment.
- 17:36 closes the loop: these very faculties will be the **witnesses against or for** the human on the Day of Judgment.
- 41:20–22 dramatizes the same: on Yawm al-Qiyamah, hearing, sight, and skins testify against their owner.
- Maududi's reading: `samīʿ` and `baṣīr` here imply **the full apparatus of intelligent, reasoning perception** — they are the human-specific gifts that distinguish *taklīf* from mere animal life.

### 9.3 Free Will and Responsibility — Implication of `Nabtalīhi`

- The verb `nabtalīhi` **presupposes** a free agent. One cannot test an automaton.
- The very next ayah (76:3) makes this explicit: `إِنَّا هَدَيْنَاهُ السَّبِيلَ إِمَّا شَاكِرًا وَإِمَّا كَفُورًا` — "We guided him to the way, whether grateful or ungrateful."
- The Quranic schema: creation (76:1–2a) → moral equipment (76:2c) → testing (76:2b, 76:3) → bifurcation into shākir / kafūr (76:3) → consequences (76:4 onward).
- This refutes both **fatalist** readings (humans as predestined puppets) and **deist** readings (Allah uninvolved in moral outcomes) — Allah creates, equips, guides, and judges; the human chooses within that ordained frame.

## 10. Philosophical / Psychological Dimension

### 10.1 The "Small Drop" as Anti-Pride Pedagogy

The framing `min nuṭfatin amshāj` functions psychologically as a **radical humility intervention**:

- The mighty human ego — capable of imperial ambition, philosophical self-aggrandizement, denial of God — is **traced back to a microscopic, anonymous, mingled droplet**.
- Sayyid Qutb's reading: the verse is a "cinematic zoom-out" from human pretension to biological origin.
- Compare 80:17–22: `قُتِلَ الْإِنسَانُ مَا أَكْفَرَهُ * مِنْ أَيِّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقَهُ * مِن نُّطْفَةٍ خَلَقَهُ فَقَدَّرَهُ` — "Cursed be man, how ungrateful is he! From what did He create him? From a nuṭfah He created him and proportioned him." The Quran repeatedly deploys the *nuṭfah* framing as **the cure for human arrogance**.
- Psychologically this maps onto well-documented effects of *small-self* / *awe* experiences: cosmic perspective reduces ego-inflation, increases moral consideration of others, fosters gratitude (cf. Keltner & Haidt's awe research, also Yaqeen Institute's *Spiritual Psychology* paper series).

### 10.2 Why "the Human" (al-insān) — Universal Address

- The use of `al-insān` (not `al-muʾminīn` or `al-nās` in this position) addresses **humanity-as-such**, not believers, not Arabs.
- Surat al-Insan is a **manifesto for the human condition**: every human, regardless of faith, was made from a mingled drop, equipped for the test.
- This universalism makes the surah's later prescriptions (feed the miskin, the yatim, the asir — vv. 8–10) **ethical-universal** in scope: the test is a human test, not a tribal one.
- The dual etymology of `insān` (forgetfulness + sociability) finds resolution in v.2: humans **forget** their origin (and thus their dependence), but are **made for connection** (sociable creatures equipped with hearing and seeing to receive and respond) — the test is whether the latter wins over the former.

### 10.3 Sociological / Philosophical / Economic / Environmental Lenses (Brief)

- **Sociological:** v.2 demolishes social-class hierarchies — every human starts identical (nuṭfah amshāj). vv.8–10's prescription to feed the destitute follows directly from the equality of origin.
- **Philosophical (Aristotelian-Islamic synthesis):** the verse mirrors Aristotle's *De Anima* ordering of the senses — hearing and sight as the two most epistemically rich modalities — but reframes them under divine bestowal rather than naturalist emergence.
- **Economic:** if every human shares the same origin and the same test, **wealth is a circumstance of the test, not a measure of worth** — anticipating the surah's later glorification of the *abrār* who give away their food.
- **Environmental:** the chain of mingling continues — the human body is made of elements drawn from the soil, water, atmosphere; embryology's `amshāj` is the local case of a cosmic principle of *mingling* sustained by divine wisdom.
