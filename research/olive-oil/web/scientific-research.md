# Scientific Research on Olive Oil — Health Benefits

> **Quranic and Prophetic Context:** The olive tree is described in the Quran as a "blessed tree" (شجرة مباركة) in Surah An-Nur (24:35), and olives are mentioned among the signs of Allah in Surah An-Nahl (16:11) and Surah At-Tin (95:1). The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said: "Eat olive oil and anoint yourselves with it, for it comes from a blessed tree" (Tirmidhi). Modern scientific research has increasingly validated the remarkable health properties of olive oil across nearly every organ system — findings that resonate deeply with the Prophetic emphasis on both consuming and applying it topically.

---

## Cardiovascular Health

### Harvard / JACC Cohort Study — Olive Oil Consumption and Mortality
- **Key findings:** Consuming more than half a tablespoon (>7g) of olive oil per day is associated with a 19% lower risk of cardiovascular disease mortality. The study also found 19% lower risk of death from any cause, along with reduced cancer, neurodegenerative, and respiratory disease mortality.
- **Journal/Institution:** Journal of the American College of Cardiology (JACC) / Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://www.jacc.org/doi/10.1016/j.jacc.2021.10.041

### Meta-Analysis of Prospective Cohort Studies — CVD and All-Cause Mortality
- **Key findings:** Higher olive oil consumption conferred a significantly reduced risk of CVD (RR: 0.85) and all-cause mortality (RR: 0.83) across 13 prospective cohort studies. Maximum benefit was observed at consumption between 20–30 g/day.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC / Frontiers (meta-analysis)
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9623257/

### PREDIMED Trial — Mediterranean Diet with EVOO
- **Key findings:** Individuals at high cardiovascular risk who consumed a Mediterranean diet supplemented with extra virgin olive oil experienced a 30% reduction in major cardiovascular events compared to a low-fat control group. The study involved over 7,000 participants.
- **Journal/Institution:** PREDIMED Research Network / New England Journal of Medicine
- **Year:** 2013 (with ongoing follow-up analyses)
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10346407/

### Systematic Review — EVOO and Cardiovascular Disease Prevention
- **Key findings:** A 2025 systematic review confirmed that EVOO consumption plays a pivotal role in preventing recurrent cardiovascular events. EVOO rich in polyphenols improves lipid profiles, reduces total cholesterol, and increases HDL cholesterol.
- **Journal/Institution:** MDPI Nutrients
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6643/17/11/1843

### JAHA Randomized Crossover Trial — EVOO in Plant-Based Diet
- **Key findings:** A randomized crossover trial examined the cardiometabolic effects of extra virgin olive oil within a whole-food plant-based vegan diet, finding positive impacts on cardiovascular markers.
- **Journal/Institution:** Journal of the American Heart Association (JAHA)
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/JAHA.124.035034

## Cancer Prevention

### Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis — Olive Oil and Cancer Risk
- **Key findings:** Meta-analysis results provide valuable evidence of the protective effects of olive oil against cancer development. Olive oil consumption appears to exert beneficial actions in terms of overall cancer prevention.
- **Journal/Institution:** PLOS ONE
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0261649

### Harvard Study — Olive Oil and Cancer Mortality
- **Key findings:** People who consume at least 1.5 teaspoons of olive oil per day have a 17% lower risk of cancer mortality. The study linked higher olive oil intake with reduced risk across multiple cancer types.
- **Journal/Institution:** Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://news.harvard.edu/gazette/story/2024/03/surprising-link-to-an-everyday-food-in-cancer-findings/

### Southern Italian Cohort — EVOO and Gastrointestinal Cancers
- **Key findings:** Individuals consuming more than 50 g/day of extra virgin olive oil experienced a 20% lower risk of all-cause mortality and a striking 60% reduction in mortality from gastrointestinal cancers.
- **Journal/Institution:** MDPI Cancers
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://www.mdpi.com/2072-6394/16/21/3575

### Hunter College — Oleocanthal Kills Cancer Cells
- **Key findings:** Oleocanthal, a phenolic compound in EVOO, was shown to kill human cancer cells within 30 minutes with no obvious effect on normal cells. The compound ruptures the cancer cells' waste-storage vesicles (lysosomes), causing cell death.
- **Journal/Institution:** Hunter College, City University of New York
- **Year:** 2015 (with ongoing research)
- **URL:** https://www.hunter.cuny.edu/news/hunter-study-shows-that-certain-olive-oils-kill-cancer-cells/

### Comprehensive Review — Oleocanthal in Inflammation and Cancer
- **Key findings:** Oleocanthal demonstrates comprehensive anti-cancer properties through multiple mechanisms including anti-proliferative, pro-apoptotic, and anti-metastatic effects. Its bioactive compounds influence gene expression and epigenetic mechanisms.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC / Antioxidants
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10741130/

## Brain & Cognitive Health

### Systematic Review — Olive Oil and Cognitive Performance
- **Key findings:** Cross-sectional studies consistently report positive associations between olive oil consumption and cognitive health across multiple cognitive domains. Virgin olive oil consumers showed improved memory, attention, and executive function over two years compared to refined olive oil consumers.
- **Journal/Institution:** Frontiers in Nutrition
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2023.1218538/full

### Auburn University — EVOO and Mild Cognitive Impairment
- **Key findings:** In a study of 25 participants with mild cognitive impairment who consumed 30 ml of olive oil daily for six months, extra-virgin olive oil resulted in improvements in blood-brain barrier function, enhanced brain function, memory, and improvements in clinical dementia rating and behavioral scores.
- **Journal/Institution:** Auburn University
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://ocm.auburn.edu/newsroom/news_articles/2023/03/060903-olive-oil-research.php

### Temple University — EVOO and Alzheimer's Disease
- **Key findings:** Extra-virgin olive oil has been shown to protect memory and learning ability, and reduce the formation of amyloid-beta plaques and neurofibrillary tangles in the brain — classic markers of Alzheimer's disease. Some antioxidant compounds in olive oil can cross the blood-brain barrier.
- **Journal/Institution:** Temple University Health System
- **Year:** 2017 (with ongoing research)
- **URL:** https://www.templehealth.org/about/news/extra-virgin-olive-oil-preserves-memory-and-protects-brain-against-alzheimers-disease-new-research-at-temple-shows

### Neuroprotective Properties of EVOO Polyphenols in Alzheimer's Disease
- **Key findings:** A multi-target mechanistic review examined the neuroprotective properties of EVOO polyphenols, identifying multiple pathways through which they protect against Alzheimer's disease progression, including reduction of neuroinflammation and oxidative stress.
- **Journal/Institution:** Frontiers in Nutrition
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2025.1736633/full

### Virgin Olive Oil, Cognition, and Gut-Brain Axis
- **Key findings:** Researchers identified a bacterial genus called Adlercreutzia as a potential marker of the beneficial link between virgin olive oil consumption and preserved cognitive function — highlighting the gut-brain axis connection. The industrial refining process strips away polyphenols essential for the brain-boosting effect.
- **Journal/Institution:** Yale School of Public Health / Oxford Academic (Nutrition Reviews)
- **Year:** 2021–2025
- **URL:** https://ysph.yale.edu/news-article/assistant-professor-tassos-c-kyriakides-discusses-the-cognitive-benefits-of-olive-oil/

## Anti-inflammatory Properties

### Oleocanthal — Natural Ibuprofen
- **Key findings:** In 2005, scientists discovered that oleocanthal in freshly pressed EVOO shares pharmacological properties with ibuprofen. It prevents production of pro-inflammatory COX-1 and COX-2 enzymes. Approximately 50 ml (3.5 tablespoons) of EVOO provides roughly 10% of the ibuprofen dosage recommended for adult pain relief.
- **Journal/Institution:** Current Pharmaceutical Design / PubMed
- **Year:** 2011 (landmark discovery 2005)
- **URL:** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21443487/

### Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis — Anti-Inflammatory Benefits
- **Key findings:** Reviewed randomized controlled trials reveal beneficial effects of olive oil by reducing levels of inflammation markers. An olive oil-rich diet has beneficial effects on C-reactive protein (CRP) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) compared with low-fat diets.
- **Journal/Institution:** Nutrition Research / ScienceDirect
- **Year:** 2019
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0899900719301182

### Olive Polyphenols — Antioxidant and Anti-Inflammatory Properties
- **Key findings:** A comprehensive review documented that olive polyphenols exhibit potent antioxidant and anti-inflammatory properties through multiple molecular pathways. EVOO polyphenols inhibit the NF-kB pathway, a master regulator of inflammation.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC / Antioxidants (MDPI)
- **Year:** 2021
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8300823/

### EVOO Polyphenols and Rheumatoid Arthritis
- **Key findings:** Extra virgin olive oil polyphenol-enriched extracts exert antioxidant and anti-inflammatory effects on peripheral blood mononuclear cells from rheumatoid arthritis patients. Human, animal, and lab experiments show polyphenols in EVOO effectively combat inflammation and improve arthritis symptoms.
- **Journal/Institution:** MDPI Antioxidants
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3921/14/2/171

### EVOO Effects on Inflammation and Microbiota
- **Key findings:** High-polyphenol EVOO consumption reduced the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-1-beta while modulating gut and saliva microbiota composition, demonstrating a systemic anti-inflammatory effect.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11940808/

## Skin & Topical Benefits

### Olive Oil Polyphenols and Human Fibroblasts
- **Key findings:** Bioactive compounds in EVOO — hydroxytyrosol, tyrosol, and oleocanthal — stimulate human fibroblast proliferation and migration, promoting wound healing and tissue regeneration. Hydroxytyrosol controls inflammatory responses of keratinocytes by inhibiting the NF-kB pathway.
- **Journal/Institution:** MDPI Nutrients
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10181161/

### Olive Oil in Dermatology — Ancient Traditions Meets Modern Medicine
- **Key findings:** Olive oil demonstrated effectiveness in reducing erythema, scaling, and pain associated with radiation and contact dermatitis, managing symptoms of atopic dermatitis and psoriasis, and promoting wound healing for pressure ulcers, chronic wounds, and burns.
- **Journal/Institution:** SKIN: The Journal of Cutaneous Medicine
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://skin.dermsquared.com/skin/article/view/2823

### Oleocanthal, Oleacein, and Skin Aging — Randomized Clinical Study
- **Key findings:** Significant wrinkle reduction was documented in a randomized clinical study: for women aged 45-79 years the mean change was -33.91%, and for men aged 45-79 years it was -46.56%. EVOO-derived compounds show clear anti-aging benefits when applied topically.
- **Journal/Institution:** Medicina (MDPI)
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://www.mdpi.com/1648-9144/60/6/947

### EVOO vs. Petrolatum — Skin Barrier Function
- **Key findings:** Both extra virgin olive oil and petrolatum significantly improved skin barrier function by increasing stratum corneum hydration and reducing erythema and skin temperature. EVOO performed comparably to standard dermatological emollients.
- **Journal/Institution:** Journal of Clinical Medicine (MDPI)
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12251406/

### Olive Oil for Hair Health
- **Key findings:** Hair treated with olive oil is less susceptible to damage than untreated hair. Olive oil's fatty acids (oleic, linoleic, palmitic) strengthen the hydrolipidic film coating hair fibers. A mouse study found that oleuropein (a bioactive compound in olive oil) can positively impact the hair growth cycle.
- **Journal/Institution:** Various (compilation of studies)
- **Year:** Various
- **URL:** https://www.oliveoil.com/olive-oil-skin-care-hair-care/

### Saudi Population Cross-Sectional Study — Olive Oil for Skin
- **Key findings:** A cross-sectional study examined the widespread use of olive oil for skin health in a Saudi population, documenting traditional and contemporary applications alongside clinical outcomes.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8991441/

## Metabolic Health

### Dose-Response Meta-Analysis — Olive Oil and Diabetes Risk
- **Key findings:** There is a 13% reduced risk of diabetes in cohort studies and 22% reduced risk in RCT studies for highest compared with lowest olive oil consumption. The highest olive oil intake category showed a 16% reduced risk of type 2 diabetes.
- **Journal/Institution:** Journal of Health, Population and Nutrition / Springer Nature
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41043-025-00866-7

### EVOO and Metabolic Diseases — Comprehensive Review
- **Key findings:** EVOO's polyphenols partially inhibit carbohydrate digestion and absorption, reduce hepatic glucose release, and increase peripheral glucose uptake. Polyphenol antioxidant activity reduces production of advanced glycosylated end-products such as HbA1c.
- **Journal/Institution:** International Journal of Molecular Sciences (MDPI)
- **Year:** 2024
- **URL:** https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/25/15/8117

### Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis — Olive Oil in Type 2 Diabetes Prevention
- **Key findings:** Olive oil intake is associated with a decreased risk of developing type 2 diabetes as well as improved glucose metabolism. The PREDIMED study confirmed that a Mediterranean diet enriched with EVOO can improve glucose metabolism, preventing diabetes onset.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC / Nutrition & Diabetes
- **Year:** 2017
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5436092/

### EVOO-Enriched Mediterranean Diet and Metabolic Syndrome
- **Key findings:** An EVOO-enriched Mediterranean diet showed clinical impact on metabolic syndrome, with improvements across multiple metabolic markers including blood glucose, lipid profiles, and inflammatory biomarkers.
- **Journal/Institution:** Frontiers in Nutrition
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.980429/full

### Olive Oil, Plasma Metabolites, and Risk of Type 2 Diabetes and CVD
- **Key findings:** EVOO showed a lower risk of diabetes than standard olive oil. EVOO contains high amounts of bioactive compounds including squalene, phytosterols, triterpenoids, carotenoids, tocopherols, and phenolic compounds that contribute to its superior metabolic protection.
- **Journal/Institution:** Cardiovascular Diabetology / BioMed Central
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://cardiab.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12933-023-02066-1

## Gut Health

### EVOO and Gut-Brain Axis
- **Key findings:** A comprehensive review examined EVOO's influence on gut microbiota, mucosal immunity, and its downstream effects on cardiometabolic and cognitive health. Olive oil compounds engage in bidirectional communication with gut microbiota — microbes transform olive oil constituents into new bioactive metabolites, while those compounds reshape the microbial community.
- **Journal/Institution:** Nutrition Reviews / Oxford Academic
- **Year:** 2021
- **URL:** https://academic.oup.com/nutritionreviews/article/79/12/1362/6133931

### Olive Oil Phenolic Compounds and Gut Microbiota Modulation at Strain Level
- **Key findings:** Olive oil polyphenol consumption increases Bacteroidetes populations and reduces the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio, both of which are associated with atheroprotection. Polyphenols also increase beneficial bacteria and gut microbial diversity, which can be therapeutic for lipid-immune disorders and obesity.
- **Journal/Institution:** Frontiers in Nutrition
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2023.1272139/full

### Portuguese Clinical Study — High-Polyphenol EVOO and Microbiome
- **Key findings:** High-polyphenol EVOO consumption increased Bacteroidota populations in both gut and oral microbiomes while reducing the pro-inflammatory cytokine IL-1-beta, demonstrating systemic benefits originating from gut health improvements.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11940808/

### Olive Oil and Colorectal Cancer Prevention via Gut Microbiota
- **Key findings:** The correlation between olive oil intake and gut microbiota composition was examined in the context of colorectal cancer prevention, finding that olive oil's modulation of gut bacteria may contribute to its chemopreventive effects.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC / Nutrients
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9504660/

### Olive-Derived Bioactives and Digestive Health
- **Key findings:** Olive-derived bioactive compounds including polyphenols, secoiridoids, and triterpenes modulate the gut microbiota and promote intestinal health. EVOO supports a healthy gut ecosystem with anti-inflammatory, antimicrobial, and hepatoprotective effects.
- **Journal/Institution:** MDPI Foods
- **Year:** 2025
- **URL:** https://www.mdpi.com/2304-8158/14/14/2413

## Longevity & General Health

### Harvard 28-Year Prospective Study — Olive Oil and Longevity
- **Key findings:** Over a 28-year period, people who consumed the most olive oil (more than half a tablespoon per day) had a 19% lower risk of death from any cause compared with those who rarely or never consumed olive oil. Higher consumption was associated with lower risks of death from cardiovascular disease, cancer, neurodegenerative disease, and lung disease.
- **Journal/Institution:** Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health / JACC
- **Year:** 2022
- **URL:** https://www.health.harvard.edu/staying-healthy/harvard-study-high-olive-oil-consumption-associated-with-longevity

### Olive Oil and Longevity — Evidence Review
- **Key findings:** A comprehensive review linking olive oil consumption to increased longevity found that the content of multiple microcomponents with antioxidant potential, combined with high monounsaturated fat content, appears essential for the life-extending effect.
- **Journal/Institution:** Annex Publishers / Journal of Nutrition and Health
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://www.annexpublishers.com/articles/JNH/12102-Olive-Oil-and-Longevity-A-Review-of-Evidence-Linking.pdf

### EFSA Health Claim — Olive Oil Polyphenols
- **Key findings:** The European Food Safety Authority (EFSA) has authorized an official health claim confirming that "olive oil polyphenols contribute to the protection of blood lipids from oxidative stress" when consuming at least 5 mg of hydroxytyrosol and its derivatives daily. This is one of only a few diet-related health claims authorized by EFSA.
- **Journal/Institution:** European Food Safety Authority (EFSA)
- **Year:** 2011 (ongoing regulatory status)
- **URL:** https://www.highphenolic.com/post/health-benefits-of-olive-oil-polyphenols

### III International Conference on Virgin Olive Oil and Health — Consensus Report
- **Key findings:** An international consensus established that virgin olive oil and its phenolic compounds have demonstrated health benefits in cardiovascular disease, cancer, neurodegeneration, metabolic syndrome, and inflammatory conditions. The report synthesized evidence from clinical trials, epidemiological studies, and mechanistic research.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC / Nutrients
- **Year:** 2019
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6770785/

### Olive Oil Consumption and Human Health — Narrative Review
- **Key findings:** A comprehensive narrative review concluded that regular olive oil consumption is associated with reduced risk of overall mortality, cardiovascular events, stroke, and metabolic disorders. The benefits are primarily attributed to the synergistic effects of monounsaturated fatty acids and polyphenolic compounds.
- **Journal/Institution:** Maturitas / ScienceDirect
- **Year:** 2018
- **URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378512218306522

## Extra Virgin vs. Refined Olive Oil

### Narrative Review — Is EVOO the Critical Ingredient?
- **Key findings:** Studies with refined olive oils (rich in monounsaturated fat but lacking phenols) show they do not provide health benefits compared to extra virgin oils. This provides strong evidence that monounsaturated fat alone is not the source of health benefits — the polyphenols are essential.
- **Journal/Institution:** PMC / Nutrients (MDPI)
- **Year:** 2023
- **URL:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10346407/

### Key Differences
- **EVOO:** Mechanically extracted without heat or chemicals; retains full polyphenol content (hydroxytyrosol, oleocanthal, oleuropein); lower smoke point; best for low-heat cooking, salads, and direct consumption
- **Refined olive oil:** Chemically processed; stripped of vitamins, polyphenols, and natural bioactive compounds; higher smoke point; suitable for high-heat cooking but with significantly reduced health benefits
- **Recommendation:** For health benefits, extra virgin olive oil is clearly superior. The optimal daily intake supported by research is 20-30 g (approximately 2 tablespoons).
