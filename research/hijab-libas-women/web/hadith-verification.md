# Hadith Verification — Women's Parda / Hijab & Libas (Dress) in Islam

> **Topic key:** `hijab-libas-women`
> **Compiled:** 2026-06-24
> **Authenticity rule:** Every entry below carries a collection name, hadith number, a **grade** (with the grader named), a plain-meaning line, and a **sunnah.com** link. Grades are taken from sunnah.com (al-Albani / Darussalam in-page gradings), and cross-checked against islamqa.info, islamweb.net, abuaminaelias.com, and hadeethenc.com where the chain is disputed. Nothing here is invented; where a famous narration is weak it is flagged in the clearly-labelled disputed/weak section.

## PART A — SAHIH / HASAN (sound and usable)

### A1. Aisha — Ansar women tore their waist-sheets to make khimar (An-Nur 24:31)
- **Collection:** صحيح البخاري — Sahih al-Bukhari
- **Number:** 4759 (In-book: Book 65, Hadith 281 — Tafsir of Surah An-Nur)
- **Grade:** **Sahih** (Bukhari's own collection — highest authenticity)
- **Plain meaning:** When *"...wal-yadribna bi-khumurihinna ʿalā juyūbihinna"* (let them draw their khimar over their bosoms — 24:31) was revealed, the women of the Ansar immediately tore their waist-wrappers (uzur) from the edges and used the cloth to cover their heads. Shows the Companions' instant obedience and that the khimar was drawn down over the chest, not merely on the head.
- **Arabic (opening):** لَمَّا نَزَلَتْ هَذِهِ الآيَةُ ‏{‏ وَلْيَضْرِبْنَ بِخُمُرِهِنَّ عَلَى جُيُوبِهِنَّ ‏}‏ أَخَذْنَ أُزْرَهُنَّ فَشَقَّقْنَهَا مِنْ قِبَلِ الْحَوَاشِي فَاخْتَمَرْنَ بِهَا
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4759
- **Note:** This is the soundest narration tying a dress command directly to a Companion-level practice. Central for both the foundation and the modern segments.

### A2. Umm Salama — Ansar women came out "like crows" after the jilbab verse (Al-Ahzab 33:59)
- **Collection:** سنن أبي داود — Sunan Abi Dawud
- **Number:** 4101 (In-book: Book 34, Hadith 82)
- **Grade:** **Sahih** (al-Albani)
- **Plain meaning:** When *"yudnīna ʿalayhinna min jalābībihinna"* (33:59) was revealed, the women of the Ansar went out so covered in dark outer garments (akisiya) that they looked like they had crows on their heads. Companion-era illustration of the jilbab being adopted at once.
- **Arabic:** لَمَّا نَزَلَتْ ‏{‏ يُدْنِينَ عَلَيْهِنَّ مِنْ جَلاَبِيبِهِنَّ ‏}‏ خَرَجَ نِسَاءُ الأَنْصَارِ كَأَنَّ عَلَى رُءُوسِهِنَّ الْغِرْبَانَ مِنَ الأَكْسِيَةِ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4101
- **Note:** Companion to A1 — A1 is the khimar/An-Nur evidence; A2 is the jilbab/Al-Ahzab evidence.

### A3. "Two types of people of Hell" — women clothed yet naked, heads like camel humps
- **Collection:** صحيح مسلم — Sahih Muslim
- **Number:** 2128 (In-book: Book 37 — Clothing & Adornment, Hadith 190); narrated by Abu Huraira
- **Grade:** **Sahih** (Muslim's own collection)
- **Plain meaning:** Two groups of Hell-dwellers the Prophet ﷺ had not yet seen: (1) people with whips like oxen-tails who beat people, and (2) women *kāsiyāt ʿāriyāt* — "clothed yet naked" — leaning/inclining toward evil and luring others, their heads swollen like the leaning humps of bukht camels; they will not enter Paradise nor smell its fragrance.
- **Arabic (key phrase):** صِنْفَانِ مِنْ أَهْلِ النَّارِ لَمْ أَرَهُمَا ... وَنِسَاءٌ كَاسِيَاتٌ عَارِيَاتٌ مُمِيلاَتٌ مَائِلاَتٌ رُءُوسُهُنَّ كَأَسْنِمَةِ الْبُخْتِ الْمَائِلَةِ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/muslim:2128
- **Note:** **Central to the modern tight/transparent-clothing discussion.** Classical commentators read *kāsiyāt ʿāriyāt* as: tight/sheer clothing that covers in name but reveals the body, and/or being clothed in worldly finery but stripped of taqwa. The "will not enter Paradise / smell its fragrance" wording is understood by Ahl al-Sunnah as severe deterrent language (not eternal kufr), per islamweb.net 271387 and Daruliftaa.

### A4. The muhrimah must not wear niqab or gloves (face-veil debate evidence)
- **Collection:** صحيح البخاري — Sahih al-Bukhari
- **Number:** 1838 (In-book: Book 28 — Penalty of Hunting while on Pilgrimage, Hadith 18); narrated by Ibn ʿUmar
- **Grade:** **Sahih** (Bukhari's own collection)
- **Plain meaning:** Listing ihram clothing rules, the Prophet ﷺ said the woman in ihram must **not** wear the niqab (face-veil) or gloves (quffazayn).
- **Arabic (key phrase):** وَلاَ تَنْتَقِبِ الْمَرْأَةُ الْمُحْرِمَةُ وَلاَ تَلْبَسِ الْقُفَّازَيْنِ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1838
- **Note — what scholars infer:** Those who hold the face is **not** ʿawrah (e.g., the Hanafi/Maliki default, and al-Albani) argue this proves niqab was a *known, normal-state* garment that ihram suspends — so it is not obligatory in itself. Those who hold niqab obligatory argue it only bans the *fitted/stitched* niqab in ihram while the face can still be covered loosely (citing A6 below). The hadith itself is sahih; the *inference* is the locus of disagreement.

### A5. The woman from Khath'am — al-Fadl ibn Abbas and the turned gaze (Farewell Hajj)
- **Collection:** صحيح البخاري — Sahih al-Bukhari (also in Sahih Muslim)
- **Number:** 1513 (In-book: Book 25 — Hajj, Hadith 1); narrated by Ibn Abbas
- **Grade:** **Sahih** (Bukhari's own collection)
- **Plain meaning:** During Hajj al-Wadaʿ a young woman of Khath'am came to ask about Hajj on behalf of her elderly father. Al-Fadl (riding behind the Prophet ﷺ) and the woman kept looking at each other, so the Prophet ﷺ physically turned al-Fadl's face away.
- **Arabic (opening):** كَانَ الْفَضْلُ رَدِيفَ رَسُولِ اللَّهِ صلى الله عليه وسلم فَجَاءَتِ امْرَأَةٌ مِنْ خَثْعَمَ ...
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1513
- **Note — face-veil debate:** Non-niqab scholars argue the woman's face was visible (al-Fadl was "looking at her"), and the Prophet ﷺ corrected the *gaze*, not her *uncovering* — so face-covering was not obligatory. Pro-niqab scholars respond that this was during ihram (where niqab is removed per A4) and/or that turning the gaze shows the danger that justifies covering. Sound text, contested inference.

### A6. "Do not follow the first glance with a second" (lowering the gaze)
- **Collection:** سنن أبي داود — Sunan Abi Dawud
- **Number:** 2149 (In-book: Book 12, Hadith 104); narrated by Buraydah, addressed to ʿAli
- **Grade:** **Hasan** (al-Albani). Also narrated via Tirmidhi 2777 / Ahmad.
- **Plain meaning:** "O ʿAli, do not follow up a (chance first) glance with a second; the first is (forgiven/excused) for you, but the second is not."
- **Arabic:** يَا عَلِيُّ لاَ تُتْبِعِ النَّظْرَةَ النَّظْرَةَ فَإِنَّ لَكَ الأُولَى وَلَيْسَتْ لَكَ الآخِرَةُ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/abudawud:2149
- **Note:** Primary evidence that the *man's* lowered gaze (Quran 24:30) shares the burden of modesty — important to balance a dress discussion that focuses only on women. The famous "first glance vs second glance" maxim.

### A7. Allah does not accept a (mature) woman's prayer without a khimar
- **Collection:** سنن أبي داود — Sunan Abi Dawud
- **Number:** 641 (In-book: Book 2, Hadith 251); narrated by Aisha. Also Tirmidhi 377 (graded hasan by Tirmidhi), Ibn Majah, Ibn Khuzaymah (sahih).
- **Grade:** **Sahih** (al-Albani)
- **Plain meaning:** Allah does not accept the salah of a woman who has reached the age of menstruation (i.e. matured) unless she wears a khimar (head-covering). Establishes covering the head/hair in salah.
- **Arabic:** لاَ يَقْبَلُ اللَّهُ صَلاَةَ حَائِضٍ إِلاَّ بِخِمَارٍ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/abudawud:641
- **Note:** Core evidence for the **dress required in salah** (Item 11). "Ḥāʾiḍ" here means one who has reached the age of menses, not one currently menstruating (she doesn't pray then). Body must be covered except — per the majority — face and hands.

### A8. The woman who perfumes herself and passes by men
- **Collection:** سنن أبي داود — Sunan Abi Dawud
- **Number:** 4173 (In-book: Book 35, Hadith 15); narrated by Abu Musa al-Ashʿari. Also Tirmidhi 2786 (sahih), Nasa'i 5126, Ibn Hibban.
- **Grade:** **Hasan** (al-Albani); graded **Sahih** by Tirmidhi, Hakim & Dhahabi.
- **Plain meaning:** If a woman wears perfume and passes by people so that they smell her fragrance, "she is such-and-such" (a strong rebuke; in the Tirmidhi/Nasa'i wording: "she is like an adulteress/zaaniyah"). Part of the modesty-of-presentation, not only cloth.
- **Arabic:** إِذَا اسْتَعْطَرَتِ الْمَرْأَةُ فَمَرَّتْ عَلَى الْقَوْمِ لِيَجِدُوا رِيحَهَا فَهِيَ كَذَا وَكَذَا
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4173
- **Note:** Useful for the modern segment: modesty includes scent/self-presentation, not just fabric coverage.

### A9. "Whoever imitates a people is one of them" (tashabbuh)
- **Collection:** سنن أبي داود — Sunan Abi Dawud
- **Number:** 4031 (In-book: Book 34, Hadith 12); narrated by Ibn ʿUmar
- **Grade:** **Hasan Sahih** (al-Albani: حسن صحيح). Ibn Taymiyyah graded the chain *jayyid*; Ibn Hibban authenticated it.
- **Plain meaning:** "Whoever imitates a people is one of them." Cited in dress discussions about adopting clothing that is a religious/identity marker of another group.
- **Arabic:** مَنْ تَشَبَّهَ بِقَوْمٍ فَهُوَ مِنْهُمْ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4031
- **Note:** Commonly over-extended in popular preaching. Scholars restrict it to imitation in matters that are *distinctive religious symbols or sinful customs* of another people, not to ordinary permissible clothing — worth nuancing in the myth-busting segment so it is not used as a blanket ban on all foreign dress.

### A10. Garment of fame/vanity (libas al-shuhrah)
- **Collection:** سنن أبي داود — Sunan Abi Dawud
- **Number:** 4029 (In-book: Book 34, Hadith 10); narrated by Ibn ʿUmar. Parallel: Ibn Majah 3606/3607.
- **Grade:** **Hasan** (al-Albani)
- **Plain meaning:** "Whoever wears a garment of fame/shuhrah (clothing worn to stand out and provoke pride or notoriety), Allah will clothe him in a garment of humiliation on the Day of Resurrection."
- **Arabic:** مَنْ لَبِسَ ثَوْبَ شُهْرَةٍ أَلْبَسَهُ اللَّهُ يَوْمَ الْقِيَامَةِ ثَوْبًا مِثْلَهُ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4029
- **Note:** Cuts both ways — applies to attention-seeking *either* by extravagance/flashiness *or* by ostentatious "holier-than-thou" dress. Relevant to the modesty/tabarruj theme (showing off, not just exposure).

### A11. Lengthening the hem — the women's exception to the isbal rule
- **Collection:** جامع الترمذي — Jami' at-Tirmidhi
- **Number:** 1731 (In-book: Book 24 — Clothing, Hadith 53); Umm Salama questioning the Prophet ﷺ
- **Grade:** **Hasan Sahih** (Tirmidhi; al-Albani: sahih)
- **Plain meaning:** When the warning against trailing garments below the ankle (for men) was given, Umm Salama asked about women. The Prophet ﷺ allowed women to let the hem down a hand-span (shibr); when she noted their feet would still show, he allowed a forearm's length (dhirāʿ) and no more — i.e. women are commanded to cover the feet/more fully, the opposite of the men's restriction.
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1731
- **Note:** Good supplementary evidence that women's covering extends to the feet, and that men's and women's dress rulings differ deliberately.

## PART B — DISPUTED / WEAK / OFTEN MIS-CITED (handle with care; useful for myth-busting)

### B1. Asma bint Abi Bakr — "nothing should be seen except this and this (face & hands)"
- **Collection:** سنن أبي داود — Sunan Abi Dawud
- **Number:** 4104 (In-book: Book 34, Hadith 85; Chapter: "What a woman may show of her beauty")
- **Grade:** **DISPUTED.** sunnah.com lists **al-Albani: Sahih**, *but* Abu Dawud himself flagged it as **mursal** (broken chain) — the narrator Khalid b. Durayk **did not meet** Aisha, so the link to Aisha is missing. Many muhaddithun therefore grade the isnad **da'if**; al-Albani considered it strengthened to the level of acceptable by supporting narrations.
- **Plain meaning:** Asma entered upon the Prophet ﷺ in thin/transparent clothes; he turned away and said that once a woman menstruates (matures), nothing of her should be seen *except this and this* — pointing to the **face and the hands**.
- **Arabic:** يَا أَسْمَاءُ إِنَّ الْمَرْأَةَ إِذَا بَلَغَتِ الْمَحِيضَ لَمْ تَصْلُحْ أَنْ يُرَى مِنْهَا إِلاَّ هَذَا وَهَذَا ‏.‏ وَأَشَارَ إِلَى وَجْهِهِ وَكَفَّيْهِ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/abudawud:4104
- **Note — IMPORTANT for the debate:** This is the single most-cited proof that the face & hands are not ʿawrah. Be honest in the document: the **chain is interrupted (mursal)**; al-Albani still graded it sahih (via corroboration) while others hold it weak. So it is *evidence with a caveat*, not an undisputed sahih. The non-niqab position does not rest on this hadith alone — it also leans on the sound A1, A2, A4, A5 above.

### B2. "The woman is ʿawrah; when she goes out the Shaytan looks toward her (adorns her)"
- **Collection:** جامع الترمذي — Jami' at-Tirmidhi
- **Number:** 1173 (In-book: Book 12 — Suckling, Hadith 28); narrated by Ibn Masʿud
- **Grade:** **DISPUTED.** Tirmidhi graded it **Hasan Gharib**; **Darussalam (sunnah.com) grades it Da'if** owing to the chain (Qatada's ʿanʿana / weakness in the path). Frequently quoted as if flatly sahih — it is not undisputed.
- **Plain meaning:** "The woman is ʿawrah (to be concealed); when she goes out the Shaytan beautifies her / casts his gaze on her to tempt people through her."
- **Arabic:** الْمَرْأَةُ عَوْرَةٌ فَإِذَا خَرَجَتِ اسْتَشْرَفَهَا الشَّيْطَانُ
- **Link:** https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:1173
- **Note:** Often deployed to argue a woman's *entire body* (including face) is ʿawrah, or even that she should not leave the house. Flag that (a) the grading is contested (hasan per Tirmidhi vs. da'if per Darussalam), and (b) even if accepted, "the Shaytan looks at her" is about temptation in the gaze, and is contradicted in practice by the many sound reports of Companion women attending the mosque, marketplace, and Hajj uncovered-faced. Good myth-busting material against the "women must never go out" misuse.

### B3. POPULAR MISQUOTES & FABRICATIONS to flag (myth-busting)

These circulate widely on social media / WhatsApp attached to parda; treat as **not establishable / weak / baseless** unless a sound chain is produced:

1. **"A woman has no obligation/right to leave her house except for the grave" / "her house is better than the mosque for her, and her grave better than her house."** — The extreme "tomb" wording is **not authentic**; the only sound related material is the mild report that a woman's prayer in the inner part of her home carries more reward (Abu Dawud 570, narrated about Umm Humayd / Ibn Masʿud), which is about *reward*, not a prohibition on leaving. The harsh "grave" phrasing is **unverifiable / rejected**. Do **not** cite it as a parda proof.

2. **"Women are deficient in intellect and religion, therefore must be veiled/secluded."** — The "deficient in intellect and religion" wording **is** in Sahih al-Bukhari 304 / Muslim 80, **but** it has **nothing to do with dress**; it explains menstruation (missed prayers/fasts) and witness-testimony context. Using it as a *hijab* or *seclusion* proof is a **misapplication**, not a fabrication — flag the misuse.

3. **"If a woman shows a strand of hair, X angels curse her" / fixed numbers of angels cursing, or fixed counts of sins, or hellfire wrapped around uncovered hair.** — These **numbered-curse / numbered-sin** narrations about hair or dress are **baseless (la asla lahu) / fabricated**; no sound chain exists. Common on social media. Reject.

4. **"A woman in tight jeans / makeup is cursed by 70,000 angels until she removes it"** and similar viral numeric warnings — **fabricated**, no isnad. The *authentic* basis for the tight/sheer-clothing critique is **A3 (Sahih Muslim 2128 — kāsiyāt ʿāriyāt)** alone; cite that, not the viral numbers.

5. **"Tabarruj like the tabarruj of the first Jahiliyyah"** is a **Quranic** phrase (Al-Ahzab 33:33), *not* a hadith — make sure it is cited as Qur'an, not narrated as a saying of the Prophet ﷺ.

> **Verification status of B3:** items 1, 3, 4 are flagged weak/baseless based on the absence of a sound chain on sunnah.com and the standard muhaddith verdicts referenced via islamqa.info / hadithanswers.com / islamweb.net; item 2 is a sound text mis-applied; item 5 is Qur'an, not hadith. Where a specific viral number is quoted, the burden of proof (a named collection + number + chain) is on the one citing it — and none exists.
