# Fiqh Reference: Women's Parda / Hijab and Libas (Dress) in Islam

> **Scope & authenticity note.** This file documents the Qur'anic texts, their classical tafseer, the asbab al-nuzul, the core fiqh question ("how much is fard?"), the niqab *ikhtilaf*, the agreed conditions of a valid covering, and contemporary garment fatwas. Every position is attributed to the actual scholar, madhhab, or institution that holds it, verified via WebSearch/WebFetch against islamqa.info, islamweb.net, seekersguidance.org, dar-alifta.org, abukhadeejah.com (al-Albani), quran.com tafsirs, and academic sources. Where scholars genuinely differ, the *ikhtilaf* is presented and labelled. Nothing here is a fabricated quote — where a narration could not be retrieved verbatim from a primary chain, it is flagged as "reported by tafseer authorities" rather than quoted as if from the source text. Researchers should still confirm exact hadith wordings against sunnah.com before publication.

## A. THE VERSES AND THEIR TAFSEER

### A.1 — Surah An-Nur 24:30–31 (lowering the gaze; the women's covering)

**Verse links:** [Quran.com 24:30](https://quran.com/24:30) · [Quran.com 24:31](https://quran.com/24:31)

**24:30 (men):** "Tell the believing men to lower their gaze (*yaghuddu min absarihim*) and guard their chastity (*wa yahfazu furujahum*). That is purer for them. Indeed, Allah is acquainted with what they do."

**24:31 (women):** "And tell the believing women to lower their gaze and guard their chastity, and **not to display their adornment (*wa la yubdina zinatahunna*) except what [ordinarily] appears thereof (*illa ma zahara minha*)**, and **to draw their head-coverings over their bosoms (*wal-yadribna bi-khumurihinna ʿala juyubihinna*)**, and not to display their adornment except to [the listed mahrams]..."

The pairing is deliberate: the command to men (lower the gaze) comes *first*, establishing that the burden of modesty is mutual and that men are addressed before women.

#### Key term 1 — *Zeenah* (adornment)
*Zeenah* = "that with which a woman adorns herself to appear attractive — nice clothes or ornaments" (Maarif-ul-Quran / Mufti Shafiʿ, [quran.com tafsir](https://quran.com/en/24:31/tafsirs/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran)). Scholars distinguish:
- **Hidden adornment (*zeenah khafiyyah*)** — jewellery on the body, the body's own beauty — must be concealed from non-mahrams.
- **Apparent adornment (*zeenah zahirah*)** — the subject of the exception "*illa ma zahara minha*."

#### Key term 2 — *"Illa ma zahara minha"* (the central ikhtilaf)
This phrase generates the single most important disagreement in the whole topic. Two classical readings, both from Companions:

| Reading | Held by | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) The outer garment only** | ʿAbdullah **ibn Masʿud**; also reported from al-Hasan al-Basri, Ibn Sirin, Ibrahim al-Nakhaʿi | "What appears" = only the outer clothing/wrap that unavoidably shows; the face and hands themselves are *not* part of the exception. This reading supports covering the face. |
| **(b) The face and hands (and per some, feet)** | ʿAbdullah **ibn ʿAbbas**; also ʿAisha, Ibn ʿUmar | "What appears" = the face and the hands up to the wrists, because "when a woman is forced to go out... it is difficult to hide face and palms while engaged in her work" (Maarif-ul-Quran). This reading supports leaving the face/hands uncovered. |

A third, custom-based reading (al-Qiffal and some early authorities): "that which a human being may openly show in accordance with prevailing custom" — invoked by some moderns to argue the exception is time-and-place sensitive.

**Critical caveat carried by both reading-(b) supporters:** even those who permit the face uncovered agree that *if there is genuine risk of fitnah* (arousal of illicit desire) in a particular context, uncovering the face is *not* allowed (Maarif-ul-Quran). This caveat is what later Hanafis lean on (see B.1).

#### Key term 3 — *Khimar* (the head-covering) and *Juyub* (the bosoms/necklines)
- ***Khimar*** (pl. *khumur*) was a pre-existing head-cloth Arabian women already wore. The Qur'an did not invent a new garment; it **re-directed an existing one**. (Ibn Kathir; Maarif-ul-Quran.)
- The pre-Islamic practice: women threw the khimar back over the shoulders/back, leaving the neck, upper chest and the slit of the dress (*jayb*) exposed. The verse commands the khimar be drawn *forward over the juyub* — the openings of the garment at the neck and bosom — so the neck and chest are covered, "so that nothing can be seen of them" (Ibn Kathir; Maarif-ul-Quran). So the verse's explicit, undisputed demand is **covering the head, neck and chest**; the face is the contested zone.

#### The list of mahrams (before whom adornment may show)
24:31 enumerates those to whom a woman need not conceal her adornment. As listed in Maarif-ul-Quran ([quran.com](https://quran.com/en/24:31/tafsirs/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran)):
1. Husband
2. Fathers (incl. grandfathers up the line)
3. Husbands' fathers (fathers-in-law)
4. Sons
5. Husbands' sons (stepsons)
6. Brothers (incl. half-brothers)
7. Brothers' sons (nephews)
8. Sisters' sons (nephews)
9. Their [Muslim] women
10. Those whom their right hands possess (female slaves)
11. Male attendants having no [sexual] desire (e.g. the very old/incapable)
12. Children not yet aware of women's private aspects (pre-pubescent)

The verse closes by forbidding women to "stamp their feet so as to make known the adornment they hide" — covering even the *sound* of anklets, underscoring how comprehensively concealment of *zeenah khafiyyah* is intended.

#### Asbab al-nuzul / context for 24:31
Reported by al-Bukhari from **ʿAisha (RA)**: "May Allah have mercy on the women of the early Muhajirat (emigrant women). When Allah revealed [the command] to draw their *khumur* over their *juyub*, they tore their *muruut* (waist-wraps/aprons) and covered themselves with them." A parallel report: when Surat al-Nur was revealed and the men recited the verse to their women, the next morning the women prayed "as if there were crows on their heads" from the dark cloths they had wrapped on. (al-Bukhari; cited in Ibn Kathir and myislam.org tafsir of 24:31. Confirm exact wording on sunnah.com.) This shows the immediate, undisputed practical effect was head-and-bosom covering.

### A.2 — Surah Al-Ahzab 33:59 (the *jilbab* verse)

**Verse link:** [Quran.com 33:59](https://quran.com/33:59)

"O Prophet, tell your wives and your daughters and the women of the believers **to draw over themselves [part] of their outer garments (*yudnina ʿalayhinna min jalabibihinna*)**. That is more suitable that **they will be recognised and not be abused/harassed (*dhalika adna an yuʿrafna fala yu'dhayn*)**. And ever is Allah Forgiving and Merciful."

#### Meaning of *jilbab*
*Jilbab* (pl. *jalabib*) = a large outer wrapping sheet worn *over* the regular clothes and the khimar. Ibn Kathir: "the *jilbab* is a *rida'* (outer wrap) worn over the *khimar*," likening it to the *izar*; al-Jawhari defines it as "the outer wrapper" ([Ibn Kathir on quran.com](https://quran.com/al-ahzab/59/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir)). So 24:31 addresses the khimar (head-cloth); 33:59 adds an *outer cloak* for going out.

#### Meaning of *"yudnina"*
From *idna'* = to draw/bring near. With the particle *ʿala* it means "to let down / draw down over." Tafseer authorities: it means to **drape the cloak down over the body**. On *how* it was applied:
- **Ibn ʿAbbas** (reported in Ibn Kathir): women were commanded "to cover their faces from above their heads with the *jilbab*, leaving only one eye showing."
- **ʿUbayda al-Salmani**, when asked to demonstrate *yudnina ʿalayhinna*, "covered his face and head, with just his left eye showing." (Ibn Kathir, [quran.com](https://quran.com/al-ahzab/59/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir).)

These narrations are the backbone of the *face-covering* (niqab-obligatory) camp's argument from 33:59.

#### Meaning of *"an yuʿrafna fala yu'dhayn"* (recognised and not harassed)
Ibn Kathir: covering this way means "it will be known that they are free women, and that they are not slave-women or *whores*" — i.e. a public marker of chaste, free, believing status, which deterred harassment. The verse is a rare case where the *ʿillah* (legal cause/wisdom) is stated *inside the verse itself*.

#### Asbab al-nuzul for 33:59
Reported by tafseer authorities (Ibn Kathir, al-Tabari): in Madinah, free women and slave women were dressed alike (a headscarf and chemise), so corrupt men / hypocrites would harass women at night — especially when they went out to relieve themselves — under the pretext of "mistaking" them for slave-women. The *jilbab* gave free believing women a distinguishing public mark so they would be recognised and left alone. (Reported from al-Dahhak and al-Suddi via the tafseer; see [Ibn Kathir on quran.com](https://quran.com/33:59/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir) and tafseer summaries.)

### A.3 — Surah Al-Ahzab 33:53 (the verse of *hijab* / the screen)

**Verse link:** [Quran.com 33:53](https://quran.com/33:53)

The relevant clause: "...And when you ask [the Prophet's wives] for something, **ask them from behind a partition/screen (*min wara'i hijab*)**. That is purer for your hearts and their hearts..."

**This is the verse where the word *hijab* literally appears in the Qur'an — and it denotes a physical *screen/curtain*, not a garment.** It is the source of the technical "verse of hijab."

#### Asbab al-nuzul for 33:53 (al-Bukhari, from Anas ibn Malik; ʿUmar's wish)
- **Anas ibn Malik (RA)** narrated (al-Bukhari): at the wedding feast (*walima*) of the Prophet ﷺ and **Zaynab bint Jahsh**, guests lingered talking after the meal, embarrassing the Prophet ﷺ, who was extremely shy/modest (*hayaa'*). The verse of the screen was then revealed, and "the Messenger of Allah drew a curtain (*hijab*) between him and me." (Sahih al-Bukhari; al-Wahidi, *Asbab al-Nuzul* — note: the altafsir.com page returned 403 on fetch; this is the well-known Bukhari wording, confirm on sunnah.com.)
- **ʿUmar ibn al-Khattab (RA)** narrated (al-Bukhari): "O Messenger of Allah, both righteous and immoral people enter upon you, so why not order the Mothers of the Believers to observe *hijab*?" — and the verse of hijab was revealed. This is one of the three matters in which "my view coincided with my Lord" (*muwafaqat ʿUmar*). (Sahih al-Bukhari.)

#### Scope: addressed to the Prophet's wives — how far does it generalise?
The verse's *direct address* is to the **Mothers of the Believers** (the Prophet's wives), who carry a heightened standard throughout Surat al-Ahzab (cf. 33:32, "you are not like any other women"). The scholarly discussion (Maarif-ul-Quran; Tafheem/Maududi, [islamicstudies.info](https://www.islamicstudies.info/tafheem.php?sura=33&verse=53&to=55)):
- **Generalising view:** the *physical screen* requirement (asking from behind a curtain) was specific to the Prophet's wives, but its *underlying principle* (separation, guarding against free mixing) is a model the whole Ummah should emulate. The general dress obligation for all women rests on 24:31 and 33:59, not on the screen of 33:53.
- **Face-covering camp** uses 33:53 as a *supporting* indicator that the Prophet's household practised the strictest separation, and reads 33:59 as extending the spirit of it to all believing women.
- The mainstream therefore does **not** derive "all women must be behind a literal curtain" from 33:53; the wives' screen is treated as their specific honour, while the universal rulings come from An-Nur and the jilbab verse.

### A.4 — Surah Al-Ahzab 33:33 (*tabarruj*)

**Verse link:** [Quran.com 33:33](https://quran.com/33:33)

"And abide in your houses and **do not display yourselves as [in] the former times of ignorance (*wa la tabarrajna tabarruja al-jahiliyyati al-ula*)**..." (Primary address: the Prophet's wives; the *prohibition of tabarruj* is taken by scholars as a general principle for all believing women.)

#### Meaning of *tabarruj*
Root *b-r-j* = to become manifest/conspicuous (cf. *burj* = tower; *barijah* = a ship whose sails show from afar). Applied to a woman, Ibn Kathir and the classical commentators give three senses ([Ibn Kathir on quran.com](https://quran.com/en/33:33/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir); Maududi):
1. Displaying the charms of her face/body to people.
2. Displaying her adornments and ornaments to others.
3. Making herself conspicuous by alluring gait, posture, and coquetry.

Cited authorities: **Mujahid, Qatadah, Ibn Abi Najih** — "to walk in a vain, alluring, coquettish manner"; **Muqatil** — "displaying necklaces, earrings and bosom"; **al-Mubarrad** — "revealing the adornments she ought to conceal"; **Abu ʿUbayda** — "making herself conspicuous by display of body and dress to attract men." *Tabarruj* is thus the conceptual opposite of valid hijab: it is the active display the conditions in Section C are designed to prevent.

## B. THE CORE FIQH QUESTION — HOW MUCH IS FARD?

The dress obligation (*satr al-ʿawrah* before non-mahrams) is **agreed upon**; the *ikhtilaf* is only about the **boundary** — specifically the face and hands. Two questions must be kept separate:
1. What is the *ʿawrah* of a free woman before non-mahram men? (Agreed: the whole body is to be covered; the dispute is whether *face + hands* are included.)
2. Is wearing the **niqab** (face-veil) **obligatory**, or **recommended/virtuous but not obligatory**?

### B.1 — Position 1 (the majority): whole body EXCEPT the face and hands

This is the position of the **Hanafi** and **Maliki** schools, and a well-known view within the **Shafiʿi** and **Hanbali** schools. The face and hands (palms, to the wrist) are *not* ʿawrah and may be uncovered before non-mahram men *absent fitnah*.

- **Hanafi:** ʿAwrah = the entire body except face, hands (palms to wrist), and — per the relied-upon Hanafi view — the **feet**. Evidence: ibn ʿAbbas's reading of "*ma zahara minha*" = face and hands. *However*, later Hanafis (al-Sarakhsi, al-Quhustani, the Deobandi tradition) hold that in an age of *fitnah*, a young woman should cover her face when going out — not because the face is ʿawrah, but to block the *means* to fitnah (*sadd al-dharaʿi*). So the Hanafi face-covering, where required, is a *precaution*, not because the face is intrinsically ʿawrah. (Deoband.org, ["The Hijab of Women and its Boundaries"](https://www.deoband.org/2010/06/theology-rulings/the-hijab-of-women-and-its-boundaries/); SeekersGuidance Hanafi answers; [IslamQA Hanafi 149138](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/darululoomtt/149138/the-awrah-of-a-woman-according-to-the-four-schools-of-thought/).)
- **Maliki:** ʿAwrah = whole body except face and hands. Malikis hold it is **disliked (makruh)** for a woman to wear the niqab where it is *not* the local custom — insisting on it against the custom of the land is treated as over-zealousness (*ghuluww*). (Dar al-Ifta, [dar-alifta.org fatwa 6766](http://dar-alifta.org/Foreign/ViewFatwa.aspx?ID=6766).)
- **Shafiʿi (a view) & Hanbali (a view):** several Shafiʿi and Hanbali authorities also hold the face and hands are not ʿawrah *in themselves* (the dispute inside these schools is precisely whether they must nonetheless be covered before non-mahrams — see B.2).

**Evidence for this position:**
- Ibn ʿAbbas's tafseer of "*illa ma zahara minha*" = the face and hands.
- The hadith of **Asma' bint Abi Bakr (RA)**: the Prophet ﷺ said, when a woman reaches puberty, "nothing of her should be seen except this and this" — pointing to his **face and hands** (Abu Dawud; the wording is widely cited though graded *mursal/da'if* by some, which the opposing camp uses to push back).
- That the woman in *ihram* is forbidden to wear the niqab/face-veil — taken to show the face is normally uncoverable, i.e. not ʿawrah.

### B.2 — Position 2 (a strong view): the face and hands MUST also be covered (niqab obligatory)

This is the **relied-upon (mu'tamad) view of the later Shafiʿi school** and a strong **Hanbali** view (attributed to Imam Ahmad, who is reported to have held "every part of her is ʿawrah, even her nails"). It is championed by **Ibn Taymiyyah**, and among contemporaries by **Shaykh Ibn Baz**, the **Saudi Permanent Committee (Lajnah)**, and (with his own nuance) **Shaykh Ibn al-ʿUthaymin**. (islamqa.info, ["Is covering the face obligatory" 11774](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/11774) and ["Is a woman's face ʿawrah" 21536](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/21536/correct-view-on-the-ruling-on-covering-the-face); [iqs.org.in](https://iqs.org.in/blog/2025/04/05/the-islamic-obligation-of-niqab-understanding-face-covering-in-islam/).)

**Evidence for this position:**
- Ibn Masʿud's reading of "*ma zahara minha*" = the **outer garment**, so the face is not exempted.
- 33:59 (*jilbab*) with the Ibn ʿAbbas / ʿUbayda al-Salmani narrations that women drew the cloak over the face leaving one eye.
- The ʿAisha hadith (Abu Dawud): when riders passed the women in *ihram*, "each of us would let her *jilbab* down from her head over her face."
- 33:53 (the screen) read as showing the Prophet's household practised face concealment / full separation.

> **NB on Ibn Taymiyyah's own reasoning:** he candidly *records* that Ibn Masʿud said the apparent adornment is the **garment** while Ibn ʿAbbas said it is the **face and hands** — i.e. he acknowledges the Companion-level *ikhtilaf* — and then argues, on balance of evidence and the principle of blocking fitnah, for covering. He does not claim the obligation is undisputed.

### B.3 — Distinguishing "niqab obligatory" vs "niqab recommended but not obligatory" — al-Albani's position

A crucial middle position, which most contemporary readers misattribute. In **Jilbab al-Mar'ah al-Muslimah fi al-Kitab wa al-Sunnah**, **Shaykh Muhammad Nasir al-Din al-Albani** held:
- Covering the face and hands is **NOT obligatory (laysa bi-wajib)**. The face and hands are "*ma zahara minha*" per Ibn ʿAbbas, Ibn ʿUmar, and ʿAisha. Everything *else* of the body must be covered before non-mahram men.
- The niqab is nonetheless **legislated, recommended and virtuous (mashruʿ, mustahabb, fadilah)** — al-Albani explicitly said "I do not deny the *sharʿi* legislation of the niqab"; he rejected *both* the claim that niqab is obligatory *and* the claim that it is a blameworthy innovation.
- **His sharpest condition:** even if the face is left uncovered, it must be **free of all cosmetic adornment** — no eyeshadow, eyeliner, blusher, lipstick, lip-gloss, nail varnish, false lashes, etc. — because displaying *zeenah* on the uncovered face contradicts 24:31. So "face uncovered" is *not* a licence to display a made-up face.

(Source: [abukhadeejah.com summary of al-Albani](https://abukhadeejah.com/the-niqab-for-the-muslim-woman-uncovering-her-face-and-hands-and-displaying-her-beautifications/); al-Albani, *The Dress Code for the Muslim Woman* [kalamullah PDF](https://kalamullah.com/Books/The%20Dress%20Code%20for%20the%20Muslim%20Women%20-%20Sh.%20al-Albani.pdf).)

### B.4 — The modern reformist / academic minority (clearly labelled minority and contested)

A small, **contested minority** of academics and reformist thinkers argue that *head-covering as a religious obligation* is not strictly mandated by the text (as opposed to the chest, which they accept is explicitly commanded). This is **outside the mainstream consensus**, which holds that covering the head, neck and chest (hijab) is obligatory. Named proponents (attributions verified):
- **Khaled Abou El Fadl** — argues Islamic law requires moral reasoning and humility, cautions against dogmatism where texts permit multiple readings; has argued covering the hair is not strictly mandatory. (Wikipedia bio; his juristic writings.)
- **Amina Wadud** (*Qur'an and Woman*) — reads dress verses through the Qur'an's broader themes of justice, dignity, *taqwa* and women's moral agency, rather than as a fixed sartorial code.
- **Asma Barlas** (*"Believing Women" in Islam*) — argues the jilbab command was tied to a 7th-century slave-owning Arabian context (distinguishing free from enslaved women) and was not meant as a universal, all-times body-veil mandate; patriarchal readings stem from human bias, not the text.
- **Ibrahim B. Syed** (New Age Islam) and similar progressive writers — argue 24:31's explicit object is the *chest/bosom*, not the hair, and that head-covering is cultural. ([newageislam.com](https://www.newageislam.com/islamic-sharia-laws/ibrahim-b-syed-new-age-islam/the-qur-mandate-hijab/d/109055).)

> **Label:** These are *minority, academically contested* readings. The overwhelming consensus of the four madhhabs and mainstream contemporary scholarship is that **covering the head, neck and chest (hijab) before non-mahram men is obligatory (fard)**; the only mainstream live dispute is the *face and hands* (the niqab question of B.1–B.3).

## C. THE AGREED CONDITIONS OF A VALID HIJAB / SATR

Whatever the position on the face, scholars converge on a checklist of conditions for a covering to be *valid satr*. The classic eight (systematised by **al-Albani** in *Jilbab al-Mar'ah al-Muslimah*, and echoed by standard fatwa sites). Sources: [Madeenah.com](https://madeenah.com/conditions-for-a-muslim-womans-jilbab/); [abukhadeejah.com leaflet](https://abukhadeejah.com/hijaab-of-the-muslim-woman-and-its-conditions-download-leaflet/); al-Albani's *Dress Code* PDF; corroborated by [islamweb 257593](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/257593/specifications-of-the-islamic-code-of-dress-for-women) and [islamqa 60131](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/60131/can-muslim-women-wear-pants).

1. **Covers the entire body** — minus what is exempted under the *ikhtilaf* of Section B (face and hands per the majority; or also covered per the niqab-obligatory view).
2. **Is not itself an adornment (*zeenah*)** — the garment must not be flashy/decorated so as to attract attention (this would defeat the purpose; cf. 24:31, "*wa la yubdina zinatahunna*").
3. **Thick / opaque, not transparent** — must not be see-through such that skin colour or the body shows. (Hadith of the "clothed yet naked" women — Sahih Muslim.)
4. **Loose, not tight / figure-hugging** — must not cling and outline the limbs or body shape (same "clothed yet naked" hadith).
5. **Not perfumed** — must not be scented when going out among men. (Hadith: "Any woman who puts on perfume and passes by people so they smell her scent, she is such-and-such [i.e. sinful]" — Abu Dawud / al-Tirmidhi.)
6. **Does not resemble men's clothing (no *tashabbuh* with men)** — "The Prophet ﷺ cursed men who imitate women and women who imitate men" (al-Bukhari). This is the basis of the trousers/pants debate (Section D).
7. **Does not resemble the specific dress of disbelievers / their religious garb (no *tashabbuh* with the kuffar)** — "Whoever imitates a people is one of them" (Abu Dawud); and the hadith of the two saffron-dyed (*muʿassafar*) garments: "These are the clothes of disbelievers, do not wear them" (Sahih Muslim, from ʿAbdullah ibn ʿAmr).
8. **Not a garment of fame / showing-off (*libas al-shuhrah*)** — "Whoever wears a garment of fame (*libas shuhrah*) in this world, Allah will clothe him in a garment of humiliation on the Day of Resurrection" (Abu Dawud, from Ibn ʿUmar; graded *hasan*, and *sahih* by al-Albani in *Sahih al-Jamiʿ*). Note: *shuhrah* covers **both** ostentatiously luxurious **and** ostentatiously shabby/odd dress worn to draw attention. (islamqa.info [answer 93](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/93).)

> Per al-Albani, conditions **6, 7 and 8** apply *universally* — at home and outside, among women and men alike — while 1–5 chiefly govern dress before non-mahram men in public. (Madeenah.com.)

## D. MODERN GARMENT FATWAS — APPLYING THE CONDITIONS

**The governing principle (state this everywhere):** Islamically there is **no sacred list of "halal garment names."** A garment is judged by whether it *meets the conditions in Section C* — loose, opaque, fully covering, not imitating men or the kuffar, not perfumed, not *shuhrah*. The *same* garment can be valid or invalid depending on cut, fabric, and what it is worn with. The name (abaya vs shalwar qameez vs trousers) is secondary to the conditions.

### D.1 — Shalwar Qameez (with / without dupatta)
- **Verdict:** Valid satr *if* the qameez is long and loose, the fabric opaque, the cut not figure-hugging, and the **head, hair, neck and chest are covered** (the dupatta must actually cover head + chest, not merely drape on the shoulders). A highly decorated/embellished shalwar qameez fails condition 2 (not an adornment in itself).
- **Source:** Ustadha Shazia Ahmad, checked by Shaykh Faraz Rabbani — SeekersGuidance: shalwar qameez + hijab is sufficient when "loose and modest" with head/hair/neck/chest covered; local custom is a legitimate factor; an abaya is "even more meritorious, as it is more modest." ([SeekersGuidance](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/general-counsel/is-it-sufficient-to-wear-a-shalwar-kameez-and-hijab-as-ones-islamic-clothing-due-to-custom/).)
- **Note on dupatta:** in South Asian custom the dupatta is the *khimar* equivalent; the common failure mode is wearing it only across the shoulders/chest while leaving the head/hair uncovered, which does not satisfy 24:31.

### D.2 — Abaya
- **Verdict:** The paradigm valid outer garment (closest to the *jilbab* of 33:59) — loose, opaque, full-length, plain. Treated as the *more meritorious / more modest* baseline by fatwa sites and as "extra" beyond the minimum. **Becomes problematic only** if it is tight/fitted, sheer, or heavily embellished/embroidered/bejewelled (then it fails conditions 2/3/4 and edges toward *zeenah*/*shuhrah*). (SeekersGuidance; islamweb.)

### D.3 — Jilbab (the outer cloak)
- **Verdict:** The Qur'anic outer garment of 33:59 itself. **However**, an outer jilbab is *not a separate obligation* if the woman's existing clothes already meet all the hijab conditions (loose, opaque, fully covering, head/chest covered). Islamweb is explicit: "The jilbab is not obligatory if the woman's clothes meet the conditions of the hijab." ([islamweb 308041](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/308041/jilbaab-is-not-obligatory-if-the-womans-clothes-meet-the-conditions-of-the-hijab).) So "jilbab" denotes a *function* (full outer coverage), achievable by an abaya, a long loose coat, etc.

### D.4 — Trousers / pants in general
- **Verdict (conditional permission):** Permissible **only if** (a) worn *under* a long, loose, concealing overgarment that covers to at least mid-calf / the trousers themselves are very loose and opaque, (b) they do **not** resemble men's trousers (*tashabbuh*), and (c) they are not worn in imitation of non-Muslims' style as an identity statement. Worn *visibly* and *alone* before non-mahram men, pants/trousers are **not** acceptable because they "show the shape of the woman's legs" and "wearing them is an imitation of men."
- **Sources:** islamqa.info citing Shaykh Ibn ʿUthaymin, Shaykh Salih al-Fawzan, and Shaykh ʿAbd al-Razzaq ʿAfifi — ʿAfifi: "If a woman wears pants and puts over them a wide garment then she will not be resembling men." The overgarment must be wide, slit-free, and reach the ankles or below (a handspan above the ankle is insufficient). ([islamqa 60131](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/60131/can-muslim-women-wear-pants).) islamweb's three conditions: worn under loose covering clothes; not resembling men's trousers; not for imitating non-Muslims. ([islamweb 5521](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/5521/conditions-to-be-met-when-a-woman-wears-pants).)

### D.5 — Jeans / skinny / tight jeans
- **Verdict:** Denim fabric is *neutral / permissible in itself* (islamweb: "jeans fabric lawful to wear"). **Skinny / tight / figure-defining jeans = impermissible** before non-mahrams — they fail conditions 3–4 (they outline the *ʿawrah*: the "clothed yet naked" hadith). Loose/baggy jeans worn under a long loose top/garment can satisfy the conditions.
- **Sources:** Shaykh Assim al-Hakeem — baggy jeans + baggy top can be acceptable *provided* not skinny/tight and not resembling men's cut; tight jeans are not. ([assimalhakeem.net](https://www.assimalhakeem.net/question-can-muslim-women-wear-baggy-jeans-and-baggy-shirts-and-tea-shirts/).) Mufti Wilayah Persekutuan (Malaysia) IRSYAD AL-FATWA: jeans permissible if loose, thick, non-transparent, non-outlining; reprehensible if they "outline and define the ʿawrah." ([muftiwp.gov.my](https://muftiwp.gov.my/en/artikel/irsyad-fatwa/irsyad-fatwa-umum-cat/3951-irsyad-al-fatwa-series-357-the-ruling-of-women-wearing-jeans).)

### D.6 — Shirt + trouser
- **Verdict:** Acceptable **only** if the shirt/top is long enough and loose enough to cover the hips/thighs and not cling, the trousers are loose & opaque, the head/chest are covered, and the overall look is not a man's outfit and not tight. A short, fitted shirt over tight trousers fails conditions 1, 3, 4, and possibly 6. (Same body of fatwas as D.4–D.5.)

### D.7 — Cut variants: A-line, cigarette pants, wide-leg, palazzo, loose vs tight
The cut is judged purely by **loose vs figure-revealing**:
- **Wide-leg / palazzo / A-line / loose-cut** → satisfy the "loose, not tight" condition (4) more readily and are the safer choices; still need head/chest cover and opacity.
- **Cigarette pants / skinny / pencil cuts** → tend to fail condition 4 (tight, outline the legs) unless fully hidden under a long loose overgarment.
- The deciding test is *not* the fashion label but: does it cling to and reveal the shape of the body? If yes → invalid before non-mahrams. (Synthesised from islamqa 60131, islamweb 5521, muftiwp; al-Albani condition 4.)

### D.8 — The "trousers for women" & *tashabbuh*-with-men question (the ikhtilaf)
There is a real *ikhtilaf* on women wearing trousers, even loose ones:
- **More permissive view** (Ibn ʿUthaymin, al-Fawzan, ʿAfifi via islamqa; islamweb): permissible *if* loose, opaque, distinct from men's trousers in cut, and worn under a covering top — because trousers per se are not exclusively men's clothing in many cultures, and the *tashabbuh* prohibition targets imitation, not the garment-type.
- **More cautious / discouraging view** (commonly the Hanafi/Deobandi and some others): discourage trousers for women generally, on the grounds that trousers originated as / strongly connote men's dress and that even loose trousers tend toward *tashabbuh* and toward revealing shape; they prefer the shalwar (which is loose by design) or full-length garments. ([islamqa.org Hanafi 45081](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askmufti/45081/trousers-for-females/).)
- The disagreement is fundamentally about **whether trousers are "men's clothing" by nature (→ tashabbuh) or merely by custom**; both sides agree tight trousers are out.

### D.9 — Dress among other women / at home / among mahrams vs before non-mahram men
- **Before non-mahram men:** the full conditions of Section C apply (the strict standard above).
- **Among other Muslim women / among mahrams / at home:** the standard relaxes to the **ʿawrah between women / before mahrams**, generally taken as **navel to knee must be covered**, with the rest (arms, hair, lower legs, etc.) permissible to show among trustworthy Muslim women and mahrams — *but* conditions **6, 7, 8** (no *tashabbuh* with men, no *tashabbuh* with kuffar's religious dress, no *libas al-shuhrah*) still apply *everywhere*, and one should not adopt indecently tight/revealing dress even among women (some scholars caution against the modern "even among women, anything goes" attitude). Looking with desire is never permitted regardless of relationship. (islamweb pants fatwa notes pants are permissible "inside or outside her home" once conditions met; al-Albani: conditions 6–8 universal. Confirm the navel-to-knee inter-women ʿawrah against a fiqh manual / SeekersGuidance for the specific madhhab.)
