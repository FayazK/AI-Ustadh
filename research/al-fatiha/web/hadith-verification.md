# Hadith Verification — Surah al-Fatiha

**Last updated:** 2026-04-28
**Primary source:** sunnah.com

This file collects the most cited hadith on Surah al-Fatiha, with references and gradings. All texts are verified against sunnah.com — never paraphrase or invent isnad chains beyond what is there.

---

## 1. Fatiha as Umm al-Kitab and the Seven Oft-Repeated (Sab' al-Mathani)

**Sahih al-Bukhari 4704** — Book of Tafseer
**Narrator:** Abu Sa'id ibn Al-Mu'alla
**Grading:** Sahih (agreed upon)
**Link:** https://sunnah.com/bukhari:4704

> Abu Sa'id ibn Al-Mu'alla narrated: While I was praying in the Mosque, Allah's Messenger called me but I did not respond to him. Later I said, "O Allah's Messenger, I was praying." He said, "Didn't Allah say: 'O you who believe! Give your response to Allah (by obeying Him) and to His Messenger?'" He then said, "Shall I not teach you the most superior Surah in the Qur'an before you leave the mosque?" Then he took me by the hand, and when he intended to leave the Mosque, I said to him, "Didn't you say to me, 'I shall teach you the most superior Surah in the Qur'an?'" He said, **"Al-Hamdu lillahi Rabbil 'alamin (i.e. Surat al-Fatiha) which is As-Sab' al-Mathani (the seven oft-repeated verses) and the Grand Qur'an which has been given to me."**

This is the foundational hadith identifying al-Fatiha as both Umm al-Qur'an (the Mother of the Qur'an) and Sab' al-Mathani — the seven oft-repeated verses referenced in 15:87.

**Cross-reference:**
- **Jami` at-Tirmidhi 2875** — https://sunnah.com/tirmidhi:2875 — Abu Hurayra narrates the Prophet (ﷺ) said about Umm al-Qur'an: "By the One in Whose Hand is my soul! The like of it has neither been revealed in the Tawrah, nor the Injil, nor the Zabur, nor in the Furqan." Grading: Hasan Sahih.

## 2. The Hadith Qudsi — Salah Divided Between Allah and His Servant

**Sahih Muslim 395a** — Book of Prayers
**Narrator:** Abu Hurayra (RA)
**Chain:** Isaac ibn Ibrahim al-Handhali ← Sufyan ibn Uyayna ← Al-Ala ← his father ← Abu Hurayra ← the Prophet (ﷺ)
**Grading:** Sahih
**Link:** https://sunnah.com/muslim:395a (also at https://sunnah.com/muslim/4/41)

> Allah the Exalted has said: **"I have divided the prayer into two halves between Me and My servant, and My servant will receive what he asks."**
>
> When the servant says: **"Al-Hamdu lillahi Rabbil 'alamin"** ("All praise is for Allah, Lord of the worlds"), Allah says: "My servant has praised Me."
>
> When he says: **"Ar-Rahmanir-Rahim"** ("The Most Gracious, the Most Merciful"), Allah says: "My servant has lauded Me."
>
> When he says: **"Maliki yawmid-din"** ("Master of the Day of Judgment"), Allah says: "My servant has glorified Me — and on one occasion He said: My servant has submitted to My power."
>
> When he says: **"Iyyaka na'budu wa iyyaka nasta'in"** ("You alone we worship, You alone we ask for help"), Allah says: "This is between Me and My servant, and My servant will have what he asks for."
>
> When he says: **"Ihdinas-siratal-mustaqim, siratal-ladhina an'amta 'alayhim, ghayril-maghdubi 'alayhim wa lad-dallin"** ("Guide us to the straight path — the path of those You have favored, not those who earned anger, nor those who went astray"), Allah says: "This is for My servant, and My servant will have what he asks for."

**Why this matters:** This is the most important hadith for tafseer of al-Fatiha — it establishes the conversational structure of the surah. Verses 1-3 are pure praise; verse 4 is glorification/submission; verse 5 is the pivot/contract; verses 6-7 are the petition. The structure is mirrored in classical ring-composition analysis.

## 3. Fatiha as Ash-Shifa — The Cure (Ruqyah)

**Sahih al-Bukhari 5736** — Book of Medicine, Chapter on Ruqya by reciting Surat al-Fatiha
**Narrator:** Abu Sa'id al-Khudri (RA)
**Grading:** Sahih
**Link:** https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5736

> Some of the companions of the Prophet (ﷺ) came across a tribe amongst the tribes of the Arabs, and that tribe did not entertain them. While they were in that state, the chief of that tribe was bitten by a snake (or stung by a scorpion). They said (to the companions), "Have you got any medicine with you or anybody who can treat with Ruqya?" The Prophet's companions said, "You refuse to entertain us, so we will not treat (your chief) unless you pay us for it." So they agreed to pay them a flock of sheep. **One of them (the Prophet's companions) started reciting Surat al-Fatiha and gathering his saliva and spitting it (at the snake-bite). The patient got cured...**
>
> When they returned to the Prophet (ﷺ), they mentioned the matter. He said, **"How do you know that Surat al-Fatiha is a Ruqya? Take (the sheep) and assign for me a share with you."**

**Cross-reference:** Sahih al-Bukhari 5737 — https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5737 — confirms the same narration with the Prophet (ﷺ) smiling and asking for a share.

**Why this matters:** This is the source of the title "ash-Shifa" (the Cure) for al-Fatiha. The companion (commonly identified as Abu Sa'id al-Khudri himself) used al-Fatiha as ruqyah without prior authorization, and the Prophet (ﷺ) endorsed it.

## 4. No Prayer Without al-Fatiha

**Sahih al-Bukhari 756** — Book of Adhan
**Narrator:** 'Ubadah ibn as-Samit (RA)
**Grading:** Sahih (agreed upon — also Muslim 394)
**Link:** https://sunnah.com/bukhari:756

> Allah's Messenger (ﷺ) said: **"Whoever does not recite al-Fatiha in his prayer, his prayer is invalid."** (In another wording: "There is no prayer for one who does not recite the opening of the Book.")

**Sahih Muslim 394** — Book of Prayers — confirms the same.
**Link:** https://sunnah.com/muslim:394

**Why this matters:** Establishes the legal obligation (rukn) of reciting al-Fatiha in every rak'ah of every salah — the foundation for why this surah is recited a minimum of 17 times per day in the obligatory prayers alone.

## 5. The Two Lights — Fatiha and the Last Verses of al-Baqarah

**Sahih Muslim 806** — Book of Prayer (Travellers)
**Narrator:** Ibn 'Abbas (RA)
**Grading:** Sahih
**Link:** https://sunnah.com/muslim:806

> While Gabriel was sitting with the Messenger of Allah (ﷺ), he heard a creaking sound above him. He lifted his head and said: **"This is a gate opened in heaven today which had never been opened before."** Then an angel descended through it and Gabriel said: "This is an angel who has come down to the earth who had never come down before." The angel greeted (the Prophet) and said: **"Rejoice in two lights given to you which have not been given to any prophet before you: Fatihat al-Kitab and the concluding verses of Surah al-Baqarah. You will never recite a letter from them without being granted (something good)."**

**Why this matters:** Establishes al-Fatiha (with the last two verses of al-Baqarah, 2:285-286) as a unique gift — a "light" (nur) — given exclusively to Muhammad (ﷺ) and his ummah. No previous prophet received them.

## Notes on Grading

All five primary hadith above are **Sahih** by consensus (Bukhari and Muslim). They form the irreducible hadith base for any tafseer of al-Fatiha. Skills using this file should treat these as fully authoritative; weaker virtue-narrations (e.g., reciting Fatiha 100 times daily for specific worldly benefits) circulate widely on devotional websites but are NOT included here because their isnad cannot be verified on sunnah.com.

For asbab al-nuzul of al-Fatiha specifically, no single sahih sabab is established — the surah is regarded as among the very early Meccan revelations, given to the Prophet (ﷺ) as a foundational gift before the bulk of the Qur'an descended. Some classical scholars (Mujahid via Tabari) held it was Madani, but the majority view is Makki, based on its presence in early Makkan salah.
