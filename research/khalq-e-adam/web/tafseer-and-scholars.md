## 1. Classical tafseer on the key ayat

### 2:30 "I am placing a khalifa on the earth"

- **al-Tabari, Jami' al-Bayan on 2:30** → Records the Ibn Abbas report (via al-Dahhak): "أول من سكن الأرض الجن، فأفسدوا فيها وسفكوا الدماء" (the jinn were the first to dwell on the earth; they corrupted it and shed blood); Allah sent Iblis with a host of angels against them, then created Adam as a khalifa who replaced them. Tabari also records al-Hasan al-Basri's reading that khalifa means successive generations replacing one another, and Ibn Ishaq's reading that it simply means an inhabitant and developer (ساكناً وعامراً) of the earth. On how the angels knew about bloodshed, Tabari records (a) Ibn Abbas: they had seen what the jinn did; (b) Qatada and others: "الله أعلمهم" (Allah informed them); and Tabari's own preferred reading is that Allah told them He was placing a khalifa whose progeny would do such things ("الله أخبرهم أنه جاعل في الأرض خليفة تكون له ذرية يفعلون كذا وكذا"). → **Source:** https://quran.com/2:30/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-al-tabari **[VERIFIED]**
- **Ibn Kathir, Tafsir on 2:30** → Khalifa means "people reproducing generation after generation, century after century," and he brings 6:165, 27:62, 43:60 and 7:169 as parallels for the successive-generations sense rather than a single individual. On the angels' knowledge: "the angels had knowledge that this creation would commit mischief," either because "Allah stated that He would create man from clay" (an inference from the nature of the creature) "or the angels understood this fact from the word Khalifah," which also means one who judges disputes between people and forbids injustice. He stresses that the angels asked to learn the wisdom, not out of envy or objection. → **Source:** https://quran.com/2:30/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**
- **al-Qurtubi, al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an on 2:30** → "خليفة يكون بمعنى فاعل، أي يخلف من كان قبله" (khalifa is in the sense of an agent: one who succeeds whoever was before him). He reports the disagreement over whether Adam succeeded the jinn or earlier generations, records the pre-Adamic jinn report ("الأرض كان فيها الجن قبل خلق آدم فأفسدوا وسفكوا الدماء، فبعث الله إليهم إبليس في جند من الملائكة"), and also calls Adam "خليفة الله في إمضاء أحكامه وأوامره" (Allah's khalifa in carrying out His judgments and commands) and "أول رسول إلى الأرض". On the angels' knowledge he lists three views: they inferred it from the word khalifa; they had witnessed the jinn's corruption; Allah had informed them. He then makes the verse a proof-text for the obligation of appointing an imam: "هذه الآية أصل في نصب إمام وخليفة". → **Source:** https://quran.com/2:30/tafsirs/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi **[VERIFIED]**
- **Fakhr al-Din al-Razi, Mafatih al-Ghayb on 2:30** → **[NOT VERIFIED this session]**: quran.com does not host al-Razi's tafsir and the alternative sites were unreachable. From general knowledge, Razi discusses both "khalifa of the jinn" and "khalifa of Allah" and raises the objection that a khalifa is needed only where the principal is absent, which cannot apply to Allah; treat this as a pointer to check in Mafatih al-Ghayb (Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya ed.) before quoting.
- **Maududi, Tafhim al-Qur'an on 2:30 (notes 36 to 39)** → Khalifa "is one who exercises the authority delegated to him by his principal"; the angels' question was inquiry, not objection: they wondered "how a species of being which had been invested with discretionary power and authority could conform with the overall order." Maududi says nothing about the physical mechanism of Adam's creation or about jinn before Adam; he only notes that the Qur'an "enlightens us to a period of man's past which is otherwise inaccessible." → **Source:** https://www.islamicstudies.info/tafheem.php?sura=2&verse=30&to=39 **[VERIFIED]**

### 2:31 "He taught Adam the names"

- **Ibn Kathir on 2:31** → Ibn Abbas: the names "that people use, such as human, animal, sky, earth, land, sea, horse, donkey, and so forth, including the names of the other species," and when asked whether that included the crudest everyday terms he said yes, "even the terms for breaking wind." Ibn Kathir's conclusion: "Allah taught Adam the names of everything, their proper names, the names of their characteristics, and what they do." So in the classical reading, language and naming begin with Adam by divine teaching. → **Source:** https://quran.com/2:31/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**

### 4:1 "from a single nafs, and created from it its mate" (and 7:189, 39:6, 6:98)

- **al-Tabari on 4:1** → "Nafs wahida" is Adam (al-Suddi: "خلقكم من نفس واحدة، فمن آدم عليه السلام"). Hawwa was created from Adam's rib: Qatada: "خلقت من آدم، من ضِلَع من أضلاعه"; al-Suddi's fuller version has Adam sleeping, waking to find a woman at his head who says she was created "so that you might find tranquility with me"; Ibn Ishaq: Allah took a rib "من شِقِّه الأيسر" (from his left side) while he slept and healed the place. Tabari records no alternative reading of the rib account. → **Source:** https://quran.com/4:1/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-al-tabari **[VERIFIED]**
- **Fakhr al-Din al-Razi reporting Abu Muslim al-Isfahani (d. 322/934), Mafatih al-Ghayb on 4:1** → Razi records the majority (Hawwa from Adam's rib), then: "وهو اختيار أبي مسلم الأصفهاني: أن المراد من قوله وَخَلَقَ مِنْها زَوْجَها أي من جنسها" (Abu Muslim's choice is that "created from it its mate" means "of its own kind"), supported by the parallels "والله جعل لكم من أنفسكم أزواجاً" (16:72) and "لقد جاءكم رسول من أنفسكم" (9:128), where "min anfusikum" cannot mean bodily extraction. Abu Muslim's own tafsir is lost; his view survives only through Razi's quotations. → **Source:** Arabic quotation of Razi as posted in the Multaqa Ahl al-Tafsir thread "هل يعتد بخلاف أبي مسلم الأصفهاني..." https://mtafsir.net/threads/...34169/ **[VERIFIED quotation; volume/page in Razi not checked]**; Wikipedia pointer for Abu Muslim's biography https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Muslim_al-Isfahani.
- **Muhammad Abduh and Rashid Rida, Tafsir al-Manar on 4:1** → al-Manar holds there is no explicit textual proof that Hawwa was created from Adam's rib; "خلق منها زوجها" is not decisive for that, and the intended meaning is "من جنسها", as in 30:21 "خلق لكم من أنفسكم أزواجاً". The hadith "the woman was created from a crooked rib" is read as figurative, compared to "خُلق الإنسان من عَجَل" (21:37, "man was created of haste"). → **Source:** search summary of the Alukah forum thread "هل آدم عليه السلام أبو حواء؟" (https://majles.alukah.net/showthread.php?t=191043); the page itself failed to load (certificate error). **[PARAPHRASE: al-Manar vol. 4 on 4:1 not read directly]**. Usama Hasan's 2011 slides separately cite al-Manar 8/308 for Rida's remark that Hindus believe Adam had a mother (two adjacent domed shrines in Varanasi) **[VERIFIED as Hasan's citation only]**.
- **Muhammad Iqbal, Reconstruction, Lecture III** → Notes that "The Qur'an omits the serpent and the rib-story altogether," and reads the omission of the rib story as a signal "that the purpose of the Quranic narration is not historical." → **Source:** The Reconstruction of Religious Thought in Islam, Lecture III (archive.org PDF, alhassanain.org edition, p. 51). **[VERIFIED]**
- **Maududi, Tafhim on 4:1 (note 1)** → "The creation of the human species began with the creation of one individual." On the mate: "The details how out of that 'being' its mate was created are not known to us." He acknowledges the rib explanation in tafsir and the Bible, then: "The Qur'an, however, is silent on the matter," and it is "better that we leave the matter in the same state of ambiguity in which it was left by God." → **Source:** https://www.islamicstudies.info/tafheem.php?sura=4&verse=1&to=1 **[VERIFIED]**

### 3:59, 15:26-29, 38:71-72, 32:7-9, 7:11, 71:17, 76:1, 2:213, 49:13, 22:5, 23:12-14

- **3:59 "The likeness of Isa with Allah is as the likeness of Adam: He created him from dust, then said to him Be, and he was"** → Used by every Sunni defender of Adam's direct creation as the single strongest verse; Tahseen N. Khan's *The Provenance of Man: A Sunni Apologetic of the Original Creation of Adam* calls 3:59 "the single-most important verse" and notes the first eighty verses of Al 'Imran were revealed around the Najran delegation's debate about Isa's paternity. → **Source:** review at https://traversingtradition.com/2024/05/02/in-defense-of-the-miraculous-creation-of-prophet-adam/ **[VERIFIED as the reviewer's summary]**. Usama Hasan's counter-reading (2011): the Qur'an affirms a natural gestation for Isa's virgin birth, "so why not a natural process for Adam?" → **Source:** Usama Hasan, "Islam & the Theory/Fact of Evolution" slides, slide "Objection 1: Miraculous Adam" (PDF at unity1.store) **[VERIFIED]**
- **32:7-9 "He began the creation of man from clay, then made his progeny from an extract of a despised fluid, then fashioned him and breathed into him of His ruh"** → Ibn Kathir: "He created the father of mankind, Adam, from clay," and the progeny reproduce "from a Nutfah which comes from the loins of men and from between the ribs of women"; the breathing of the ruh gave "hearing, sight and the sense of deduction." → **Source:** https://quran.com/32:7/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**. Yasir Qadhi and Nazir Khan (Yaqeen 2018) read 32:7-8 as the verse that "distinguishes Adam's origin (from clay) from descendants' procreation." → **Source:** https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/human-origins-part-1-theological-conclusions-and-empirical-limitations **[VERIFIED]**
- **7:11 "We created YOU, then fashioned YOU, then We said to the angels: prostrate to Adam"** → Ibn Kathir: the ayah "refers to Adam, although Allah used the plural in this case, because Adam is the father of all mankind," by analogy with Allah addressing the Children of Israel about favors done to their forefathers ("since that was a favor given to the forefathers, and they are their very source, then the offspring have also been favored by it"). He refers back to his discussion at the start of al-Baqarah. → **Source:** https://quran.com/7:11/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**. Iqbal quotes exactly this verse as textual authority for reading "Adam" "more as a concept than as the name of a concrete human individual." → **Source:** Reconstruction, Lecture III, p. 51 **[VERIFIED]**. Modern evolution-friendly readers (Usama Hasan's slides; the "Guided Evolution" essay on Surah Nuh at thequran.love) make the plural into a hint of a collective creation; the classical explanation above is the mainstream one.
- **71:17 "And Allah caused you to grow from the earth as a growth (nabatan)"** → Ibn Kathir: "This (Nabat) is a verbal noun (for emphasis) and its usage here is most excellent"; 71:18 "then He will return you into it" means death, and "bring you forth" is the resurrection. → **Source:** https://quran.com/71:17/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**. Iqbal cites this verse for "man is not a stranger on this earth." → Reconstruction, Lecture III **[VERIFIED]**.
- **76:1-2 "Has there not come upon man a period of time when he was nothing mentioned? We created man from a drop of mixed fluid"** → Ibn Kathir: Allah "brought man into existence after he was not even a thing worth mentioning, due to his lowliness and weakness"; "mixed fluid" is the man's and the woman's fluids. The famous report that Adam lay as clay for forty years and 'Umar's wish "would that this had been completed" are not in the quran.com Ibn Kathir excerpt on 76:1; do not attribute them to Ibn Kathir without checking. → **Source:** https://quran.com/76:1/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**
- **15:26-29 and 38:71-72 (bashar from clay; "when I have fashioned him and breathed into him of My ruh, fall prostrate")** → Keller cites 38:71-76 for Adam's "distinct creation process" from clay with the divine breath; the Yaqeen Khan/Qadhi paper lists 15:28 among the verses whose "diversity of nouns, adjectives, and verbs" (clay, dried clay, black mud, sticky clay, dust) they say resists a metaphorical reinterpretation. → **Sources:** Keller letter https://masud.co.uk/islam-and-evolution-a-letter-to-suleman-ali/ **[VERIFIED]**; Yaqeen paper above **[VERIFIED]**. Dr. Israr Ahmed's well-known bashar/insan distinction on these verses: **[NOT VERIFIED]**, see section 3.
- **2:213 "Mankind was one ummah"** → Ibn Kathir reports Ibn Abbas: "There were ten generations between Adam and Nuh, all of them on the religion of Truth. They later disputed so Allah sent the Prophets as warners and bringers of glad tidings," with Ibn Abbas's reading "The people were one Ummah and they then disputed." → https://quran.com/2:213/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**. **49:13 "We created you from a male and a female and made you peoples and tribes"; 22:5 and 23:12-14 (embryonic stages)** → No special ikhtilaf located; 49:13 is the standard proof that all humans share one origin and honor is by taqwa. **[standard tafsir]**

### 17:85 "They ask you about the ruh; say the ruh is of the amr of my Lord"

- **Ibn Kathir on 17:85** → Records Ibn Mas'ud's hadith that Jews asked the Prophet about the ruh and he waited for revelation, notes the tension with the Makkan dating of the surah, and concludes that "the essence of the Ruh and its rulings" are matters whose detailed knowledge Allah kept to Himself; he quotes al-Suhayli that the ruh is subtle like air flowing through the body, but does not fix a definition. → **Source:** https://quran.com/17:85/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**
- **al-Ghazali, Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din, Book 21, Kitab Sharh 'Aja'ib al-Qalb** → "Ruh" has two meanings: (1) "a subtle vapor brought to maturity by the heat of the heart," rising from the heart's cavity and carried through the arteries; its diffusion in the body is like "the movement of the lamp in the parts of the house"; this is the physicians' sense and not Ghazali's concern; (2) "the subtle knowing, perceiving thing" (al-latifa al-'alima al-mudrika) in man, which "is what God referenced" in "Say: The Spirit is of the bidding of my Lord," "a marvelous divine bidding of which most minds and understandings are unable to grasp the real meaning." Ghazali makes the same two-level distinction for qalb (organ vs. latifa rabbaniyya ruhaniyya), nafs and 'aql. → **Source:** McCarthy's translation appendix, http://www.ghazali.org/articles/mccarthy-qlb.doc **[VERIFIED]**
- **Ibn al-Qayyim, Kitab al-Ruh** → Argues against thinking the soul is of the genus of bodies "in the sense that if it occupies a place, it cannot be at another place. This is a clear mistake; rather, the soul is high above the heavens and then it is returned to the grave..." (quoted in an islamweb fatwa on the book's contents). His fuller positions, that the ruh is a body of a different nature from the visible body (luminous, subtle, living, mobile), and that nafs and ruh are one entity named differently according to its states, are the standard summary of the book but were **[NOT VERIFIED verbatim this session]**. → **Source for the quotation:** https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/325312/contents-of-kitaab-ar-rooh-by-ibn-al-qayyim **[VERIFIED]**; full text at https://archive.org/details/KitabAlRuhTheSoulsJourneyAfterDeathByIbnalQayyim.
- **al-Razi's catalogue of opinions on the ruh (Mafatih al-Ghayb on 17:85)** → **[NOT VERIFIED]** this session. Related but verified: Razi, Mafatih al-Ghayb 21/37-38, rejects defining the human being by bodily form ("the consideration of the physical form in determining the meaning of what is human is false from both directions," because angels take human form without humanness and transformed beings keep humanness without human form) and attributes to the majority of theologians, including al-Ghazali, the view that the human being is neither the body nor anything physical. → **Source:** quoted in Jalajel, Yaqeen 2018, notes 25-27 **[VERIFIED]**
- **Maududi, Tafhim on 17:85 (note 103)** → Rejects the common reading that ruh here means "life"; "the word rūḥ here signifies either revelation or the angel who communicates revelation," following Ibn Abbas, Qatada and al-Hasan al-Basri ("The ruh [here] stands for Gabriel, and the question in fact was how he came down and how he conveyed revelation"). The question the Makkans asked was "What is the source of the Qur'an?" → **Source:** https://islamicstudies.info/tafheem.php?sura=17&verse=85&to=88 **[VERIFIED]**
- **al-Farahi / Amin Ahsan Islahi / Javed Ahmad Ghamidi on "amr"** → **[NOT VERIFIED]**. The Farahi-Islahi school (see https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farahi_school) reads the surah in context; Maududi's reading above (ruh = wahy/Jibril, amr = Allah's command) is the reading Islahi's Tadabbur-e-Qur'an also takes, per general knowledge, but the Tadabbur text was not consulted.
- **Other senses of ruh in the Qur'an** (ruh al-qudus and Jibril; "We revealed to you a ruh from Our amr" 42:52; Isa as "a ruh from Him" 4:171; the ruh with the angels on Laylat al-Qadr 97:4), and the nafs verses (91:7-10 the nafs and what proportioned it; 39:42 Allah takes the souls at death and in sleep; 89:27-30 the nafs at peace) → These are the standard cross-references every tafsir gives; Ibn Kathir's note on 17:85 lists "the human soul, Jibril, a great angel, the Qur'an" as the candidate meanings. **[VERIFIED for Ibn Kathir's list; the rest is Qur'anic text]**

## 2. Classical positions on beings before Adam, and on "were there humans before Adam"

- **Ibn Abbas via al-Tabari (2:30)** → The jinn dwelt on earth before Adam, corrupted it and shed blood; Iblis was sent with angels against them. → https://quran.com/2:30/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-al-tabari **[VERIFIED]**. Status: reported as a Companion's statement; the islamqa.info fatwa notes such narrations "seem to be sourced from Historical [Israeli] Narrations." **[VERIFIED]**
- **Ibn Kathir, al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya (opening chapters)** → "Many scholars of Tafsir say that the jinn were created before Adam, and before them on Earth there were the hinn and the binn," and "Allah caused the jinn to prevail over them, so they killed them and expelled them from the earth." → **Source:** quoted at https://islamqa.info/en/answers/72470 **[VERIFIED quotation]**; English of al-Bidaya at https://www.kalamullah.com/Books/Early%20Days%20-%20Ibn%20Katheer.pdf.
- **Status of the hinn/binn reports** → islamqa.info: "this is a story that cannot be attributed to any sound chain of narrators," and it cites al-Tahir ibn 'Ashur's suggestion that these may derive from "myths of the Persians and Greeks propagated by storytellers." The fatwa's conclusions: two scholarly views exist on whether jinn preceded Adam on earth; and "There is nothing in the Quran or Sunnah to indicate that there were any people living on Earth before Adam." → https://islamqa.info/en/answers/72470 **[VERIFIED]**; see also https://hadithanswers.com/inhabitants-of-the-earth-before-the-jinn/ ("these Ahadith are not correct") **[VERIFIED as search snippet]**.
- **Ibn 'Arabi, al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya** → Reports a vision at the Ka'ba during tawaf in which a man claiming to be his ancestor said he had died "forty-two thousand years" earlier; when Ibn 'Arabi objected that Adam himself was not so old, the man asked "which Adam, this one nearest to you or another?", and Ibn 'Arabi recalled a hadith "that Allah created one hundred thousand Adams." → **Source:** al-Futuhat al-Makkiyya, Dar al-Kutub al-'Ilmiyya ed., vol. 6, p. 369, as cited (with Arabic) at https://islamsalvationfromhell.blogspot.com/2025/08/ibn-arabi-100000-adams.html **[PARAPHRASE: volume/page from the blog; the Ibn Arabi Society's "Paradox of the Kaaba" article https://ibnarabisociety.org/articles/paradox-of-the-kaaba.html discusses the same vision]**. Label: a Sufi visionary report; the "100,000 Adams" hadith has no known isnad in the Sunni collections. Usama Hasan's 2011 slides quote the same passage ("At the Ka'bah, I met and spoke to a man who was not from Bani Adam") **[VERIFIED as Hasan's citation]**.
- **Shi'i hadith tradition: al-Shaykh al-Saduq, Kitab al-Tawhid** → A report attributed to Imam Muhammad al-Baqir that Allah "created a million worlds and a million Adams (ألف ألف عالم وألف ألف آدم); you are in the last of those worlds and those Adams." → **Source:** al-Tawhid, chapter 38, hadith 2, p. 271, as cited on the blog above **[PARAPHRASE]**. Usama Hasan's slides cite the same idea from al-Alusi's Ruh al-Ma'ani (a Sunni tafsir) reporting al-Baqir and Ja'far al-Sadiq: "Perhaps you think that God never created a human being other than yourselves. Nay, but God created a million Adams and you are the last of those Adams." **[VERIFIED as Hasan's citation; location in Ruh al-Ma'ani not found]**. Label: non-Sunni provenance, isolated, not used by Sunni theologians as evidence. See also Jalajel's note that al-Tha'labi (al-Kashf wa'l-Bayan 4/49) and al-Qurtubi (6/129) record Ja'far al-Sadiq's rejection of the sibling-marriage account and his suggestion that Allah sent down houris and jinn women for Adam's sons **[VERIFIED in Jalajel, note 39]**.
- **al-Suyuti on "many Adams"** → **[NOT VERIFIED]**; no text located this session.
- **Ibn Khaldun, Muqaddimah, Chapter 1, Sixth Prefatory Discussion (on the graded chain of creation)** → Rosenthal translation: "One should then look at the world of creation. It started out from the minerals and progressed, in an ingenious, gradual manner, to plants and animals. The last stage of minerals is connected with the first stage of plants, such as herbs and seedless plants. The last stage of plants, such as palms and vines, is connected with the first stage of animals, such as snails and shellfish which have only the power of touch. The word 'connection' with regard to these created things means that the last stage of each group is fully prepared to become the first stage of the next group ... The animal world then widens, its species become numerous, and, in a gradual process of creation, it finally leads to man, who is able to think and to reflect. The higher stage of man is reached from the world of the monkeys, in which both sagacity and perception are found, but which has not reached the stage of actual reflection and thinking. At this point we come to the first stage of man. This is as far as our (physical) observation extends." → **Source:** The Muqaddimah, trans. Franz Rosenthal, abridged ed. (Princeton, 2005), p. 75, as quoted in Usama Hasan's 2011 slides **[VERIFIED wording; page from Hasan]**. ## 4. Contemporary scholarly spectrum on Adam and evolution

### Shoaib Ahmed Malik's taxonomy (Islam and Evolution: Al-Ghazali and the Modern Evolutionary Paradigm, Routledge 2021)

- **Definitions (Malik's own words in his Theological Puzzles essay):** the four positions are differentiated by "acceptance/rejection of common ancestry" for (a) non-humans, (b) humans, (c) Adam.
  - **Creationism:** common ancestry is rejected entirely (no evolution for non-humans, humans, or Adam).
  - **Human Exceptionalism (HE):** "Common ancestry is true except for human beings. In this perspective, Adam is considered the first member of humankind, and since he was created miraculously, him and his entire progeny are exempted from the process of evolution."
  - **Adamic Exceptionalism (AE):** Adam and Eve are "isolated miracles"; the text leaves open "the hermeneutic possibility and compatibility of there being pre/co-Adamic humans on earth when Adam was created miraculously." Non-humans and humans (as a biological population) evolved; only Adam and Eve did not.
  - **No Exceptions (NE):** all organisms, including humans and Adam, descend through evolutionary processes.
- **Malik's verdict:** within an Ash'ari framework (God's omnipotence as "what is logically or metaphysically possible"; occasionalism, "God is the sole efficient cause of all phenomena"; radical contingency), "creationism, human exceptionalism, and Adamic exceptionalism are scripturally compatible," while he rejects "no exceptions" because he remains "committed to the miraculous creations of Adam and Eve." He keeps "a stance of theological non-commitment" among the three compatible positions, but notes that "Adamic exceptionalism is the closest alignment one can get with evolution" if the science holds. The hermeneutic engine is al-Ghazali's rule (Faysal al-Tafriqa, Qanun al-Ta'wil) that the apparent sense (zahir) of a text may be abandoned only when a demonstrative proof (burhan) shows it impossible; Malik argues that evolutionary biology's evidence cannot demonstrate the impossibility of a single miraculously created couple, so the zahir of Adam's creation stands, whereas nothing in scripture asserts that no other humans existed. → **Sources:** Shoaib Ahmed Malik, "Is Evolution Even Possible in Islamic Thought?", Theological Puzzles (2022), https://www.theo-puzzles.ac.uk/2022/05/02/malik2/ **[VERIFIED]**; review in Reading Religion https://readingreligion.org/9780367364137/islam-and-evolution/ (403 at fetch; the Goodreads/ResearchGate blurb: "al-Ghazali's hermeneutics rule out the no exceptions option in favor of Adamic exceptionalism as the position that most satisfactorily relates al-Ghazali's understanding of Muslim scripture to evolutionary theory") **[VERIFIED blurb]**; open-access PDF of the book at OAPEN https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/id/72580ef5-67da-4221-9b99-d2cbe369feab/9780367364137_text.pdf (bot-blocked at fetch).

Note the logical space Jalajel opens (section 4): the classical texts speak of the people on earth "at the time the scripture speaks to them"; whether "the earth" in 71:26 is the whole planet or the known land is a question the classical mufassirun did not pose.

### The argument from 37:77 and the three-sons hadith, and the counter-argument

- **For universal descent:** 37:77 "We made his progeny the survivors (al-baqin)" plus Tirmidhi 3931 (hasan) and the Ibn Abbas / Qatada reports (all people from Nuh) → sources above **[VERIFIED]**.
- **Against:** 37:77 says the survivors were Nuh's progeny, which is true of the ark's company relative to the drowned nation without deciding whether other peoples existed elsewhere; the Qur'an also says the believers "whom We carried with Nuh" (17:3) had offspring, so even the text's own "survivors" are not Nuh's sons alone (Maududi note 44) **[VERIFIED for Maududi]**; and the three-sons hadith is a single hasan narration through al-Hasan from Samura, treated by Ibn Kathir as genealogical lore rather than creed. This counter-argument is the modern regionalists' reasoning (Ghamidi; Naik's "his people" reading) rather than a classical position.

### Nuh's age (29:14) and the 950 years

- **Ibn Kathir on 29:14** → The 950 years are the period before the flood in which Nuh called his people. Ibn Abbas: "Nuh received his mission when he was forty years old, and he stayed among his people for a thousand years less fifty; after the Flood he lived for sixty years until people had increased and spread" (total c. 1,050). → https://quran.com/29:14/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir **[VERIFIED]**. Other reports (sent at 480; lived 350 after) circulate in Tabari and al-Bidaya **[NOT VERIFIED]**.
- **Mainstream reading of the number** → Literal: a divinely granted long life, an exception like Adam's thousand years (Tirmidhi 3368, see section 7). No classical mufassir was found reading 950 non-literally; modern non-literal readings (e.g., "years" as lunar months or a symbolic figure) exist in reformist writing but none was located with attribution this session **[NOT VERIFIED]**.
- **"Sanah" then "'aam" in 29:14** → The lexicographer al-Raghib al-Isfahani (al-Mufradat fi Gharib al-Qur'an, entry س ن ه) is the classical source for the claim that "sanah" is used of a year of hardship and drought and "'aam" of a year of ease and plenty, citing 29:14 as illustration; Razi and Zamakhshari are usually said to attribute the change to elegant variation (tafannun) and to the Qur'an's habit of stating a number by subtraction to make it vivid → **[PARAPHRASE from general knowledge; Razi's page could not be fetched and al-Mufradat was not opened]**. ### The "6,000 years since Adam" chronology and the "ten qarn" report

- **Where the 6,000-year figure comes from** → It is a Biblical-derived count (Genesis genealogies as compiled by Jewish and Christian chronographers), not a Qur'anic or Prophetic datum. al-Tabari opens Tarikh al-Rusul wa'l-Muluk with a comparison of the Jewish, Christian and Persian reckonings of the time from Adam to the Hijra (on the order of 4,600 to 6,000 years) and the report that the world's total span is 7,000 years, explicitly marking these as the reports of ahl al-kitab and of the Persians. → Franz Rosenthal, trans., *The History of al-Tabari, vol. 1: General Introduction and From the Creation to the Flood* (SUNY Press, 1989) **[PARAPHRASE; figures and pages NOT VERIFIED this session]**. Ibn Kathir, al-Bidaya wa'l-Nihaya, records the disagreement among ahl al-kitab on the count **[PARAPHRASE]**. al-Suyuti's treatise al-Kashf 'an mujawazat hadhihi al-ummah al-alf (in al-Hawi li'l-Fatawi) argues from reports that the ummah's span would not exceed 1,500 years after the Prophet within a 7,000-year world **[PARAPHRASE]**. Yasir Qadhi's 2022 statement above ("that's your narrative of Christians and Jews, it's not our narrative") is the clearest contemporary Sunni statement that the 6,000-year figure is not Islamic **[VERIFIED]**.
- **"Between Adam and Nuh were ten qarn, all on the religion of Truth" (Ibn Abbas)** → Verified in Ibn Kathir on 2:213 (https://quran.com/2:213/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir) **[VERIFIED]**; also in al-Hakim's Mustadrak and Ibn Hibban **[not checked]**. ### The historian's material (with academic sources)

- **Mesopotamian flood traditions** → Sumerian flood story (Eridu Genesis; hero Ziusudra, c. 1600 BC tablet); Atra-hasis (Old Babylonian, c. 18th century BC), in which "only a single human family survives"; Gilgamesh Tablet XI (Utnapishtim), which largely borrows the flood from Atra-hasis. → W. G. Lambert and A. R. Millard, *Atra-hasis: The Babylonian Story of the Flood* (Oxford 1969; repr. Eisenbrauns 1999); Andrew George, *The Epic of Gilgamesh* (Penguin 1999) and *The Babylonian Gilgamesh Epic* (OUP 2003); summarized with references at https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_myth **[VERIFIED summary]**.
- **Woolley at Ur (1929)** → Leonard Woolley's excavation at Ur revealed "a 60 cm (24 in) yellow layer of alluvial sand and clay, indicating a flood"; scholars read it as a local river inundation, and similar deposits at other Mesopotamian sites "all dat[e] to different periods," consistent with periodic Tigris-Euphrates flooding rather than one universal event. → Woolley, *Ur of the Chaldees* (1929); Wikipedia "Flood myth" **[VERIFIED summary]**. This is also the archaeology Maududi's note 46 points to.
- **Black Sea deluge hypothesis** → Ryan, Pitman et al., "An abrupt drowning of the Black Sea shelf," Marine Geology 138 (1997); William Ryan and Walter Pitman, *Noah's Flood: The New Scientific Discoveries about the Event that Changed History* (Simon & Schuster, 1998): a catastrophic Mediterranean inflow through the Bosporus c. 5600 BC ("50 km3 of water poured through each day ... for at least 300 days"), flooding c. 100,000 km2. Critics: Aksu et al., "Persistent Holocene Outflow from the Black Sea to the Eastern Mediterranean Contradicts Noah's Flood Hypothesis," GSA Today (2002); Yanko-Hombach et al., eds., *The Black Sea Flood Question* (Springer 2007); Aksu and Hiscott, Earth-Science Reviews (2022): "insufficient evidence for a flood scenario," gradual outflow more likely, "no archaeological evidence of humans evacuating the area." Status: contested, not consensus. → https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_deluge_hypothesis **[VERIFIED summary with citations]**.
- **Ubiquity of flood myths** → Flood narratives occur across Mesopotamia, the Hebrew Bible, Greece (Deucalion), India (Manu), China, the Americas and Oceania; Maududi (note 47 on 7:59) uses this ubiquity as evidence that the survivors' descendants carried the memory as they dispersed **[VERIFIED for Maududi]**; historians read it as independent memories of regional floods and of post-glacial sea-level rise, or as diffusion → Wikipedia "Flood myth" and the works above **[VERIFIED summary]**.
