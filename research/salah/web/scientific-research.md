# Salah — Scientific & Psychological Research

**Fetched:** 2026-05-30
**Sources:** PubMed/PMC, SAGE Journals, Journal of Physical Therapy Science, Applied Psychophysiology & Biofeedback (Springer), Basic & Clinical Neuroscience, ResearchGate (preprints/PDFs), J. Alternative & Complementary Medicine. All findings linked. Honesty flags applied per item.

> **Read this first — how to use these findings.** The studies below are mostly small (5–30 participants), pilot-stage, or narrative reviews. They are genuinely interesting and suggestive, but they are *not* proof of anything theological, and several popular online claims about salah are scientifically weak or unsupported. In the episode, treat science as a layer of *wonder and supporting reflection*, never as "proof" that the religion is true. Worship in Islam is commanded by Allah and its value does not depend on a lab result. See the closing note.

## 1. Salah movements as physical activity / musculoskeletal effects

**1a. Salat as a therapeutic / light exercise (narrative review).**
Reviews of the prayer cycle (qiyam → ruku → sujood → julus) argue that the repeated positions take most major joints and muscle groups through a regular daily range of motion, comparable to light/mild-intensity exercise or gentle physiotherapy, and that frequency (five times daily) matters as much as intensity. — *Salat (Muslim prayer) as a therapeutic exercise* and *Muslim Prayer – A New Form of Physical Activity: A Narrative Review*.
Link: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331224621_Salat_Muslim_prayer_as_a_therapeutic_exercise · https://www.academia.edu/79553554/Muslim_Prayer_A_New_Form_of_Physical_Activity_A_Narrative_Review
**Flag: preliminary** (narrative reviews, not controlled trials; "comparable to light exercise" is a reasonable framing, not a measured equivalence).

**1b. Muscle activation (EMG) during prayer positions.**
Electromyography studies (Safee, Rabbi, Ghazali and colleagues) recorded activation of back, trunk, leg and arm muscles across salat positions, finding measurable engagement of the erector spinae, trapezius, and lower-limb muscles — confirming the postures load real muscle groups rather than being inert. — Rabbi, Ghazali et al., *Investigation of the EMG activity of erector spinae and trapezius muscles during Islamic prayer*, J. Back and Musculoskeletal Rehabilitation (2018); related EMG/trunk-muscle papers by the same groups.
Link: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3233/BMR-170988 · https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337532198_Activation_of_the_trunk_muscles_during_Salat_Muslim_Prayer
**Flag: solid** (direct EMG measurement) **for the narrow claim that salat positions activate these muscles**; **preliminary** for any claim about long-term fitness/therapeutic outcomes.

**1c. Salat and the knee — randomized trial after surgery.**
A single-blinded randomized controlled trial examined salat activity on knee pain, range of motion, and muscle strength after total knee arthroplasty — a rare RCT in this area, useful because it tests salat-like movement in a clinical rehab context rather than just describing it.
Link: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/367794877_Effect_of_Salat_Activity_on_Knee_Pain_Range_of_Motion_and_Muscle_Strength_After_Total_Knee_Arthroplasty_A_Single-Blinded_Randomized_Controlled_Trial
**Flag: preliminary** (single small RCT; do not generalize; deep flexion in sujood/julus can be hard or inadvisable for some knee patients — this is a nuance, not a blanket benefit).

> **Caution for the episode:** It is fair to say "the prayer postures gently exercise the body and keep joints mobile." It is *not* fair to say "salah cures back pain / replaces physiotherapy." Some positions (deep knee flexion) may be unsuitable for certain conditions.

## 2. Sujood (prostration) and physiology / the brain

**2a. EEG alpha waves + autonomic balance during salat — the strongest single study here.**
Doufesh, Ibrahim, Ismail & Wan Ahmad recorded EEG and ECG in **30 healthy Muslim men** before, during and after salat. During prayer they found a significant rise in **relative alpha-wave power in the occipital and parietal regions** (a relaxed-but-alert state) together with a shift in heart-rate variability toward **increased parasympathetic and decreased sympathetic activity** — i.e., a measurable relaxation response. They suggest regular salat may promote relaxation, lower anxiety, and reduce cardiovascular risk. — *Effect of Muslim Prayer (Salat) on α Electroencephalography and Its Relationship with Autonomic Nervous System Activity*, J. Alternative & Complementary Medicine, 2014; and earlier *EEG Spectral Analysis on Muslim Prayers*, Applied Psychophysiology & Biofeedback, 2011.
Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4086364/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24827587/ · https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21965118/
**Flag: solid-but-small** (real physiological measurements, peer-reviewed; n=30, all male, healthy — so it generalizes cautiously).

**2b. Prostration (Sajdah) and prefrontal brain activity — pilot.**
Yousefzadeh et al. measured prefrontal EEG before/after a ~10-second sajdah in a **pilot of just 5 people (3 women, 2 men)**, finding changes in brain-activity measures (beta/gamma power, entropy, fractal dimension) — but the effects *differed by gender* and were inconsistent. Authors explicitly flag the tiny sample, few electrodes, and that they did *not* record during the actual prostration. — *The Effect of Prostration (Sajdah) on the Prefrontal Brain Activity: A Pilot Study*, Basic & Clinical Neuroscience, 2019.
Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6712630/
**Flag: preliminary** (explicitly a pilot; conflicting gender results; cannot support strong claims).

**2c. "More blood flows to the brain during sujood" — popular claim.**
The idea that the head-below-heart position floods the brain with extra blood (improving memory, preventing strokes, etc.) is widespread online but rests largely on plausibility and small/indirect data, not on direct, well-replicated cerebral-blood-flow measurement during prayer. Briefly lowering the head does increase cerebral perfusion transiently, but the elaborate health claims built on it are overstated.
Link (example of the popular framing): https://knowledgedunes.com/2025/05/10/4-physical-benefits-facts-about-sujud-prostration/
**Flag: popular-claim, not well supported** (use only as "people find this beautiful to reflect on," not as established science).

**2d. "Grounding / static-electricity discharge in sujood" — popular claim.**
The claim that touching the forehead to the ground "discharges static electricity / harmful electromagnetic build-up" or delivers "grounding/earthing" health benefits is very popular in Muslim social media. The grounding/earthing literature itself is small, contested, and considered weak by mainstream science; applying it to sujood is speculative.
Link (example of the framing, comparing sujud to grounding): https://youthneuropsychology.com/2025/06/13/prayer-peace-and-the-brain-the-psychological-and-neurological-impact-of-islamic-rituals/
**Flag: popular-claim, NOT well supported** (do not present as fact in the episode).

## 3. Salah and mental health

**3a. Salat and mental-health outcomes (clinical-studies mini-review & systematic review).**
A mini-review of clinical studies and a separate systematic review ("Salah as Medicine") report associations between regular salat and lower anxiety, depression and stress, and higher hope/life-satisfaction — attributing this to salat's meditative focus, rhythm, routine, and the relaxation response (cf. the alpha/parasympathetic findings in §2a). Both note the literature is uneven in quality. — *Investigating the effect of Salat on mental health: a mini review* (2024); *Salah as Medicine: A Systematic Review* (2025).
Link: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/380890916_Investigating_the_effect_of_Salat_Muslim_prayer_on_mental_health_in_the_results_of_clinical_studies_A_mini_review · https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394355976_Salah_as_Medicine_A_Systematic_Review_of_Physical_and_Mental_Health_Benefits_of_Islamic_Prayer
**Flag: preliminary-to-moderate** (consistent direction across many studies, but many are correlational/cross-sectional — they cannot prove salah *causes* better mental health vs. religiosity, community, and lifestyle confounds).

**3b. Mindfulness (khushu) parallels.**
Studies framing khushu (focused presence in prayer) as structurally similar to secular mindfulness report associations between mindful/khushu-rich prayer and better mental health; one exploratory study looked at prayer + mindfulness among immigrant/refugee Muslim women. — *Mindfulness in Salah Prayer and its Association with Mental Health*; *Prayers and Mindfulness... Muslim Women in the USA*, J. Religion and Health, 2022.
Link: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316957984_Mindfulness_in_Salah_Prayer_and_its_Association_with_Mental_Health · https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10943-022-01600-x
**Flag: preliminary** (mostly correlational; the mindfulness analogy is a useful bridge, not an identity).

**3c. Quran recitation/listening and anxiety/stress.**
A scoping review found recitation and listening to the Quran associated with reduced anxiety, stress and depression across many studies — relevant because recitation is integral to salah, though most studies are of variable quality. — Quran-recitation scoping review, PMC.
Link: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10704108/
**Flag: preliminary** (heterogeneous study quality).

## 4. Routine & chronobiology (the five fixed time-anchors)

**4a. Consistent daily timing supports circadian health.**
Mainstream chronobiology is clear that the body clock "thrives on routine": aligning sleep, activity and meals to consistent daily times supports circadian rhythm, mood and metabolic health. Salah is not studied as a circadian intervention, but its five fixed daily anchors (notably the pre-dawn Fajr and the day's natural rhythm) plausibly act as recurring time-cues and a powerful habit-formation scaffold. — NPR/Stanford/Franciscan Health summaries of circadian-routine science.
Link: https://www.npr.org/2025/11/03/nx-s1-5590198/health-habits-circadian-rhythms-body-clock · https://studentaffairs.stanford.edu/sleep-corner-circadian-rhythms-and-routines-cultivate-consistency-improved-mental-health
**Flag: solid for the general principle** (routine/consistent timing helps circadian health) **but indirect/inferred for salah specifically** — no study shows salah's schedule itself improves circadian outcomes. Present as a reasonable analogy, not a measured result.

**4b. Habit formation.**
The five-times-daily structure is a textbook example of habit cues (fixed times, fixed sequence, fixed location) that behavioral science links to durable routine — again, an inference applied to salah, not a salah-specific trial.
**Flag: inferred / framing.**

## 5. Wudu (ablution) and hygiene

**5a. Wudu and nasal-cavity bacterial load.**
A study at high-altitude Taif, Saudi Arabia, compared nasal-cavity bacterial content before treatment (~66%) with after saline inhalation (~21%, the gold standard) and after wudu (~29%), concluding that ablution meaningfully reduces nasal bacterial load — supporting wudu (esp. nose-rinsing, *istinshaq*) as personal hygiene. — *Ablution (Wudu) health benefits... nasal-cavity bacterial content... Taif, KSA*, J. Applied Biotechnology & Bioengineering.
Link: https://medcraveonline.com/JABB/ablution-wudu-health-benefits-hbs-through-comparison-nasalndashcavity-nc-bacterialndashcontent-bc-with-goldndashstandard-gs-at-highndashaltitude-ha-area-taif-ksa.html
**Flag: preliminary** (single small study, lower-tier journal). The broader principle that **washing hands, face, mouth and nose reduces microbial load and infection risk is solid general hygiene science** — that part is uncontroversial; the specific "wudu nearly matches saline" figure is preliminary.

## Closing note — do not overstate the science to "prove" religion

1. **Direction of reasoning.** Muslims pray because Allah commanded it (Qur'an and Sunnah), not because a 2014 EEG paper found alpha waves. If tomorrow a study found *no* effect, the obligation and the worship would be unchanged. Science here is a supporting *sign* (آية) that invites wonder — not the foundation of the ruling.
2. **Small / preliminary evidence.** Most studies above are pilots, narrow samples (often healthy men), or narrative reviews. Correlation studies (mental health) cannot prove salah *causes* the benefit, given community, lifestyle and faith confounds.
3. **Separate solid from popular.** Solid-ish: muscle activation in postures (EMG), the alpha/parasympathetic relaxation response (Doufesh, n=30), general hygiene of washing. Popular-but-weak: "static-electricity grounding in sujood," elaborate "extra brain blood = prevents strokes" claims. Do **not** state the weak claims as fact.
4. **Tone for the episode:** "Interestingly, researchers have *begun* to find..." / "Some early studies *suggest*..." — never "science has *proven* that salah...".
